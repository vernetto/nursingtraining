# Fractures and Dislocations
## Comprehensive Training Course for Health Professionals

---

## Course Purpose

This course provides health professionals with a systematic approach to the **assessment, stabilization, initial management, and definitive-treatment principles of fractures and dislocations throughout the body**.

The course emphasizes:

- Recognition of fractures and dislocations
- Trauma assessment
- Neurovascular examination
- Immobilization and splinting
- Recognition of life- and limb-threatening complications
- Safe patient handling and transport
- Principles of reduction
- Indications for urgent specialist intervention
- Imaging
- Definitive orthopedic treatment
- Rehabilitation

> **Safety principle:** Practical reduction, traction, casting, and other invasive or potentially harmful procedures should be taught under appropriate clinical supervision and according to local protocols. The course must emphasize when *not* to manipulate an injury.

# Module 19 — Dislocation Management

## 19.1 General Principles

For every suspected dislocation:

1. Assess the patient
2. Assess neurovascular status
3. Look for associated fracture
4. Provide analgesia
5. Obtain appropriate imaging when indicated
6. Determine whether urgent reduction is required
7. Perform reduction only within appropriate scope of practice
8. Reassess neurovascular status
9. Immobilize
10. Obtain post-reduction imaging when appropriate

# Module 20 — Reduction Principles

## 20.1 Principles

- Understand anatomy
- Identify contraindications
- Identify associated fractures
- Assess neurovascular status
- Provide appropriate analgesia/sedation
- Use controlled techniques
- Avoid excessive force
- Stop if resistance or complications occur
- Reassess after reduction

## 20.2 When Not to Attempt Reduction

Examples include situations involving:

- Suspected complex fracture-dislocation
- Entrapped fracture fragments
- Significant neurovascular compromise requiring specialist intervention
- Open injury
- Unclear diagnosis
- Inadequate training or equipment
- Failed previous reduction attempts

# Module 26 — Clinical Decision-Making Framework

For every suspected fracture or dislocation, the clinician should systematically ask:

### 1. What happened?

Determine the mechanism and energy of injury.

### 2. What is injured?

Identify possible involvement of:

- Bone
- Joint
- Ligament
- Tendon
- Muscle
- Nerve
- Blood vessel
- Skin and soft tissue

### 3. Is the patient physiologically stable?

Perform the appropriate trauma assessment.

### 4. Is there a life-threatening problem?

Prioritize:

- Airway
- Breathing
- Circulation
- Major hemorrhage

### 5. Is there a limb-threatening problem?

Look for:

- Ischemia
- Severe vascular injury
- Compartment syndrome
- Open fracture
- Severe soft-tissue compromise

### 6. Is there neurological injury?

Perform and document a neurological examination.

### 7. Is the fracture open?

If yes, initiate appropriate open-fracture management.

### 8. Is the injury unstable?

Determine whether urgent orthopedic intervention is required.

### 9. Does the injury require reduction?

Determine whether reduction is:

- Urgent
- Appropriate
- Safe
- Within the clinician's scope of practice

### 10. How should it be immobilized?

Select the appropriate method.

### 11. What imaging is required?

Select appropriate imaging based on:

- Anatomy
- Mechanism
- Clinical findings
- Suspected complications

### 12. Where should the patient go?

Determine whether the patient requires:

- Routine outpatient care
- Urgent orthopedic assessment
- Emergency department
- Trauma center
- Vascular surgery
- Neurosurgery
- Immediate operative management

### 13. What needs to be reassessed?

Always reassess:

- Pain
- Circulation
- Motor function
- Sensation
- Swelling
- Skin condition
- Alignment

# Core Principle

> **A fracture or dislocation is not simply a broken bone or displaced joint. The clinician must simultaneously consider the bone, joint, skin, soft tissues, nerves, blood vessels, and the patient's overall physiological condition.**

The fundamental sequence is:

**Assess → Stabilize → Examine Neurovascular Status → Immobilize → Image → Reassess → Refer/Treat Definitively**
