# Emergency Wound and Trauma Care

## A Practical Manual for First Responders and Health Professionals

> **Purpose:** Provide a structured framework for the assessment and initial management of traumatic injuries, including wounds, hemorrhage, penetrating trauma, fractures, chest injuries, burns, amputations, and shock.
>
> **Scope:** The manual distinguishes between procedures appropriate for first aid, advanced prehospital care, and definitive hospital treatment.

| Problem | Immediate priority |
|---|---|
| Massive bleeding | Direct pressure / appropriate wound packing / tourniquet |
| Open fracture | Control bleeding, cover wound, immobilize |
| Suspected fracture | Immobilize and reassess circulation |

# Core Principle

> **The first responder's job is not to make the injury look normal.**
>
> The priority is to:
>
> 1. Keep the patient alive.
> 2. Control life-threatening bleeding.
> 3. Maintain the airway and breathing.
> 4. Prevent further injury.
> 5. Protect wounds and exposed tissues.
> 6. Immobilize injuries when appropriate.
> 7. Recognize shock and other life-threatening complications.
> 8. Prevent hypothermia.
> 9. Monitor the patient continuously.
> 10. Get the patient to definitive medical care as quickly and safely as possible.

## Important Scope Limitation

Procedures such as **bullet extraction, surgical wound exploration, fracture reduction, internal fixation, vascular repair, fasciotomy, and deep debridement** belong in the section on **definitive medical/surgical treatment**, rather than being presented as first-aid procedures.

A professional manual should always identify the provider's required level of training, equipment, authorization, and scope of practice for each intervention.
