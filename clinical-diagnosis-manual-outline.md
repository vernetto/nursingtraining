# Clinical Diagnosis: A Practical Manual for History, Examination and Differential Reasoning

## Part I: Foundations of Clinical Reasoning
1. **How diagnosis works.** Pattern recognition vs hypothetico-deductive reasoning, probability, pre-test and post-test odds, and the role of prevalence.
2. **Cognitive biases and diagnostic error.** Anchoring, premature closure, availability, confirmation bias, and debiasing strategies.
3. **Building a differential diagnosis.** Anatomical, pathophysiological and "VINDICATE"-style frameworks, the "can't miss" list, and ranking by likelihood and danger.
4. **Using tests wisely.** Sensitivity, specificity, likelihood ratios, thresholds for testing and treating, and when not to test.
5. **Diagnosis with limited resources.** Relying on history and examination, clinical scoring rules, and treating empirically.

## Part II: The Clinical Encounter
6. **Taking the history.** Open and closed questions, chief complaint, history of present illness, and the SOCRATES and OLDCARTS frameworks.
7. **Past, family, social and medication history.** Occupation, travel, exposures, allergies, substance use, and adherence.
8. **Communication and trust.** Interpreters, culture, sensitive topics, and collateral history.
9. **Documentation.** SOAP notes, problem lists, and clinical handover.

## Part III: Physical Examination Skills
10. **General inspection.** Appearance, nutrition, hydration, pallor, jaundice, cyanosis, clubbing, edema, lymph nodes, and skin.
11. **Vital signs as diagnostic tools.** Interpreting trends, shock index, early warning scores, and the unwell-looking patient.
12. **Cardiovascular examination.** Pulses, JVP, heart sounds, murmurs, and signs of heart failure.
13. **Respiratory examination.** Work of breathing, percussion, auscultation, and signs of consolidation, effusion and pneumothorax.
14. **Abdominal examination.** Palpation, organomegaly, peritonism, bowel sounds, hernias, and rectal and pelvic examination.
15. **Neurological examination.** Mental status, cranial nerves, motor, sensory, reflexes, cerebellar signs, and gait.
16. **Musculoskeletal examination.** Joints, spine, range of motion, and special tests.
17. **Head, eyes, ears, nose, throat and mouth.**
18. **Skin, hair and nails.** Describing lesions and recognizing patterns.
19. **Genitourinary and breast examination.**
20. **Mental state examination.**

## Part IV: Symptom-Based Diagnosis (Presenting Complaints)
21. **Fever.** Acute, prolonged and recurrent; source identification.
22. **Chest pain.** Cardiac, pulmonary, gastrointestinal and musculoskeletal causes.
23. **Dyspnea and cough.**
24. **Headache and dizziness.**
25. **Abdominal pain.** By location, with emergencies and acute abdomen.
26. **Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea and constipation.**
27. **Gastrointestinal bleeding and jaundice.**
28. **Palpitations, syncope and collapse.**
29. **Back and joint pain.**
30. **Weight loss, fatigue and anorexia.**
31. **Edema, swelling and lumps.**
32. **Urinary symptoms and hematuria.**
33. **Altered consciousness, confusion and seizures.**
34. **Rash and skin complaints.**
35. **Bleeding, bruising and anemia.**
36. **Pain assessment** across ages and cultures.

## Part V: Recognizing the Sick Patient
37. **The first minute.** ABCDE assessment and immediate life threats.
38. **Shock.** Hypovolemic, septic, cardiogenic and obstructive; bedside differentiation.
39. **Sepsis recognition.** qSOFA, SIRS, and local adaptations.
40. **Respiratory failure and airway compromise.**
41. **Acute neurological emergencies.** Stroke, meningitis, status epilepticus, raised intracranial pressure.
42. **Acute abdomen, trauma and hemorrhage.**
43. **Metabolic emergencies.** Diabetic ketoacidosis, hypoglycemia, electrolyte disturbances, and poisoning.
44. **Deterioration and early warning.** Subtle signs that precede collapse.

## Part VI: System-Based Diagnosis of Common Diseases
45. **Cardiovascular.** Hypertension, ischemic heart disease, heart failure, arrhythmias, valvular disease, and venous thromboembolism.
46. **Respiratory.** Pneumonia, asthma, COPD, tuberculosis, pleural disease, and pulmonary embolism.
47. **Gastrointestinal and hepatic.** Peptic disease, hepatitis, cirrhosis, pancreatitis, appendicitis, and inflammatory bowel disease.
48. **Endocrine and metabolic.** Diabetes, thyroid disease, adrenal disorders, and obesity.
49. **Renal and urological.** Acute kidney injury, chronic kidney disease, urinary tract infection, and stones.
50. **Hematology and oncology.** Anemias, leukemias, lymphomas, and warning signs of cancer.
51. **Neurology.** Stroke, epilepsy, neuropathies, dementia, and Parkinson disease.
52. **Musculoskeletal and rheumatology.** Arthritis, gout, lupus, and osteoporosis.
53. **Infectious diseases.** HIV, sexually transmitted infections, malaria and other tropical fevers, and common viral illnesses.
54. **Dermatology.** Eczema, psoriasis, infections, drug eruptions, and skin cancers.
55. **ENT and ophthalmology.** Red eye, vision loss, hearing loss, sinusitis, and otitis.
56. **Psychiatry.** Depression, anxiety, psychosis, delirium, substance use, and suicide risk.

## Part VII: Special Populations
57. **Pregnancy and obstetrics.** Normal findings, danger signs, pre-eclampsia, and bleeding.
58. **Newborns and infants.** Neonatal danger signs, jaundice, and feeding problems.
59. **Children.** Developmental assessment, IMCI, fever, respiratory illness, and dehydration.
60. **Adolescents.**
61. **Older adults.** Atypical presentations, frailty, falls, polypharmacy, and delirium vs dementia.
62. **Immunocompromised patients.**
63. **Palliative and end-of-life assessment.**

## Part VIII: Investigations in Support of Diagnosis
64. **Laboratory tests.** Blood count, chemistry, inflammatory markers, liver and renal function, urinalysis, and cultures.
65. **ECG interpretation.** Systematic approach and common patterns.
66. **Chest and abdominal X-ray.** Systematic reading and typical findings.
67. **Ultrasound at the bedside.** Focused cardiac, lung, abdominal and obstetric views.
68. **Point-of-care and rapid tests.** Glucose, malaria, HIV, pregnancy, troponin, and urine dipstick.
69. **Choosing and interpreting tests in context.** Avoiding over-testing and false positives.

## Part IX: Clinical Decision Tools
70. **Scoring systems and rules.** Wells, CURB-65, CHA2DS2-VASc, Ottawa rules, and Centor criteria.
71. **Flowcharts and algorithms** for common presentations.
72. **Red flags and referral criteria.**
73. **Follow-up and safety-netting.** What to tell patients and when to reassess.

## Part X: Practice and Professional Development
74. **Case-based learning.** Worked cases from presentation to diagnosis.
75. **Clinical judgment under uncertainty.** Watchful waiting, working diagnoses, and revisiting assumptions.
76. **Ethics and patient-centered diagnosis.** Disclosure, shared decision-making, and consent.
77. **Teaching and self-assessment.** Deliberate practice, audits, and peer review.
