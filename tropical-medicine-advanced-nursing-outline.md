# Tropical Medicine for the Advanced Practice Nurse: Chapter Outline

## Part I: Foundations
1. **Scope and history of tropical medicine.** Neglected tropical diseases (NTDs), the global burden, and One Health.
2. **Epidemiology and determinants.** Climate, vectors, poverty, migration, urbanization, and the effects of climate change on disease distribution.
3. **Vectors and transmission ecology.** Mosquitoes, sandflies, tsetse, triatomines, ticks, snails, and water- and soil-borne transmission.
4. **The advanced nurse's role.** Scope of practice, task-shifting, working in low-resource settings, ethics, and cultural competence.

## Part II: Clinical Approach
5. **Travel and migration history.** Structured exposure history, incubation periods, and geographic risk mapping.
6. **The febrile returning traveler.** Triage, red flags, and a differential by incubation period.
7. **Syndromic approach.** Fever with rash, fever with jaundice, fever with neurological signs, eosinophilia, diarrhea, and skin lesions.
8. **Diagnostics.** Microscopy, rapid diagnostic tests, serology, PCR, point-of-care testing, and interpreting results where resources are limited.

## Part III: Vector-Borne Diseases
9. **Malaria.** Plasmodium species, severe malaria, treatment regimens, resistance, chemoprophylaxis, and special populations (pregnancy, children).
10. **Arboviruses.** Dengue, chikungunya, Zika, yellow fever, West Nile, Japanese encephalitis, and tick-borne encephalitis.
11. **Leishmaniasis.** Visceral, cutaneous, and mucocutaneous forms.
12. **Trypanosomiasis.** Chagas disease and human African trypanosomiasis.
13. **Filarial diseases.** Lymphatic filariasis, onchocerciasis, and loiasis.
14. **Rickettsioses and other vector-borne bacteria.** Scrub typhus, spotted fevers, and borreliosis.

## Part IV: Helminths and Protozoa
15. **Soil-transmitted helminths.** Ascaris, hookworm, Trichuris, and Strongyloides, including hyperinfection risk.
16. **Schistosomiasis and food-borne trematodes.**
17. **Cestodes.** Neurocysticercosis and echinococcosis.
18. **Intestinal protozoa.** Amoebiasis, giardiasis, and cryptosporidiosis.

## Part V: Bacterial, Viral and Fungal Infections
19. **Enteric infections.** Typhoid, cholera, shigellosis, and traveler's diarrhea, with oral rehydration and fluid management.
20. **Mycobacterial diseases.** TB (including drug-resistant TB), leprosy, and Buruli ulcer.
21. **Zoonoses.** Leptospirosis, brucellosis, rabies, anthrax, and melioidosis.
22. **Viral hemorrhagic fevers.** Ebola, Marburg, Lassa, and Crimean-Congo, including isolation and PPE.
23. **Neglected bacterial and skin diseases.** Trachoma, yaws, scabies, and tungiasis.
24. **Tropical mycoses.** Histoplasmosis, mycetoma, and sporotrichosis.
25. **Envenomation and tropical injuries.** Snakebite, scorpion, and spider bites.

## Part VI: Special Populations and Cross-Cutting Issues
26. **Pregnancy, neonates, and children.**
27. **HIV, TB, and malaria co-infection.** Immune reconstitution syndrome.
28. **Nutrition and tropical medicine.** Malnutrition, anemia, and micronutrient deficiencies.
29. **Immunocompromised and chronic patients.**
30. **Migrant and refugee health.**

## Part VII: Prevention and Public Health
31. **Vaccination.** Travel vaccines, yellow fever, typhoid, rabies, Japanese encephalitis, dengue, and malaria vaccines (R21, RTS,S).
32. **Pre-travel consultation.** Risk assessment, chemoprophylaxis, and counseling.
33. **Vector control and WASH.** Bed nets, insecticides, water, sanitation, and hygiene.
34. **Mass drug administration and elimination programs.**
35. **Surveillance, outbreak response, and notification.**

## Part VIII: Practice and Professional Development
36. **Antimicrobial stewardship and resistance** in tropical settings.
37. **Critical care in resource-limited settings.** Sepsis, shock, and severe malaria and dengue management.
38. **Palliative care and chronic sequelae.**
39. **Research and evidence appraisal.**
40. **Global health ethics, humanitarian and field work.**

## Part IX: Core Clinical Skills for Remote Practice
41. **Emergency obstetrics and newborn care.** Normal delivery, postpartum hemorrhage, eclampsia, obstructed labor recognition, neonatal resuscitation, and kangaroo care.
42. **Trauma, wound care, and minor surgery.** Suturing, abscess drainage, fracture splinting, burns, chest drains, and hemorrhage control.
43. **Anesthesia, sedation, and pain management.** Local blocks, ketamine sedation, and analgesia with limited drugs.
44. **Pediatrics and severe acute malnutrition.** IMCI protocols, dehydration, and therapeutic feeding.
45. **Chronic disease care at a distance.** Hypertension, diabetes, epilepsy, asthma, and HIV with interrupted follow-up and drug supply.
46. **Mental health and psychosocial support.** mhGAP-style care, trauma, substance use, and psychosis.
47. **Point-of-care ultrasound and basic laboratory skills.** Obstetric, FAST, and lung ultrasound; malaria, Hb, glucose, urine, HIV, and syphilis tests.
48. **Dental, eye, and ENT basics.** Extractions, abscesses, eye infections, and foreign bodies.

## Part X: Decision-Making Under Constraint
49. **Triage and referral.** Who must be evacuated, who can be treated locally, and who receives palliative care because transfer is impossible or futile.
50. **Protocol-based practice.** Working from WHO IMAI, MSF guidelines, and similar tools, and knowing when to deviate.
51. **Clinical uncertainty without diagnostics.** Reasoning without CT, lab, or a second opinion.

## Part XI: Logistics and Operations
52. **Drug and supply management.** Forecasting, stock-outs, expiry, controlled substances, and vaccine cold chain.
53. **Evacuation planning and communication.** Transport options, radio or satellite links, agreements with the nearest hospital, and costs.
54. **Power, water, and waste.** Solar power and refrigeration basics, water treatment, and sharps and infectious waste disposal.
55. **Infection prevention with improvised resources.** Sterilization, isolation, and outbreak containment.
56. **Telemedicine.** Quality photos, structured case summaries, and working with remote specialists.

## Part XII: People and Systems
57. **Training and supervising community health workers.**
58. **Language, culture, and community trust.** Traditional healers, gender dynamics, and local language.
59. **Data, registers, and reporting.** Surveillance, notifiable diseases, and basic program monitoring.
60. **Legal and professional issues.** Actual scope of practice by country, prescribing rights, liability, documentation, and malpractice cover.
61. **Funding and negotiation.** Working with NGOs, ministries, and local authorities.

## Part XIII: Personal Sustainability
62. **Security and field safety.** Vehicles, travel, and conflict zones.
63. **Your own health.** Malaria prophylaxis, vaccines, and post-exposure protocols.
64. **Burnout and moral injury.** Processing patient losses that a hospital might have prevented.
65. **Knowing your limits.** Backup, peer support, and avoiding overreach.
