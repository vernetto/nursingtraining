# Part VI: System-Based Diagnosis of Common Diseases

## Chapter 45. Cardiovascular Diseases

### Learning objectives

After studying this chapter you should be able to:

1. Diagnose hypertension accurately (office, home and ambulatory measurement), identify secondary causes and end-organ damage, and select treatment and targets.
2. Recognise hypertensive emergencies and manage them safely.
3. Diagnose stable coronary disease and acute coronary syndromes, choose investigations by pre-test probability, and outline treatment and secondary prevention.
4. Diagnose heart failure (HFrEF, HFmrEF, HFpEF), identify the cause and precipitants, and apply the four pillars of therapy and device indications.
5. Classify atrial fibrillation, assess stroke and bleeding risk, and choose rate or rhythm control and anticoagulation.
6. Recognise and grade the major valvular lesions, and know the indications for intervention, endocarditis diagnosis, and anticoagulation for prosthetic valves.
7. Diagnose and manage venous thromboembolism (DVT and PE), including risk stratification and duration of anticoagulation.
8. Recognise cardiomyopathies, myocarditis, pericardial disease, pulmonary hypertension, peripheral arterial disease, and aortic aneurysm.

### 45.1 Hypertension

#### Definition and measurement

**Hypertension** is persistently elevated blood pressure. Thresholds differ slightly between guidelines (ESC/ESH: office ≥140/90 mmHg; ACC/AHA 2017: ≥130/80 mmHg). A practical approach: **diagnose** on repeated measurements ≥140/90 mmHg in the office **confirmed** by out-of-office measurement, then treat toward a lower individualised target.

| Method | Threshold for hypertension |
|---|---|
| **Office (clinic)** | ≥140/90 mmHg (average of ≥2 readings on ≥2 visits) |
| **Ambulatory BP monitoring (ABPM) (gold standard)** | Daytime average ≥135/85; 24-hour ≥130/80; night-time ≥120/70 |
| **Home BP monitoring (HBPM)** | ≥135/85 (average over 4–7 days, two readings morning and evening) |

**Correct technique:** seated and rested 5 minutes, back supported, arm at heart level, correct **cuff size** (undersized cuffs overestimate), no caffeine, smoking or exercise for 30 minutes, **both arms at the first visit** (use the higher arm; a difference >15 mmHg suggests subclavian or aortic disease), standing BP in older adults and diabetics (orthostatic hypotension). **White-coat hypertension** (high office, normal ABPM) and **masked hypertension** (normal office, high ABPM/home) are common; ABPM/HBPM identify them.

**Grades (office):** high-normal 130–139/85–89; grade 1 140–159/90–99; grade 2 160–179/100–109; grade 3 ≥180/110.

#### Causes

- **Essential (primary) hypertension:** about 90–95%; multifactorial (genetics, obesity, salt intake, alcohol, inactivity, ageing, stress, sleep apnoea).
- **Secondary hypertension (5–10%):** suspect when **onset <30 years (or >60 with sudden onset), severe or resistant hypertension, sudden worsening, spontaneous hypokalaemia, grade 3, target organ damage out of proportion,** or clinical clues.

| Cause | Clues and tests |
|---|---|
| **Renal parenchymal disease** (CKD, glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidneys) | Raised creatinine, proteinuria, haematuria; renal ultrasound |
| **Renovascular** (atherosclerotic in older smokers; **fibromuscular dysplasia in young women**) | Flash pulmonary oedema, abdominal bruit, creatinine rise on ACE inhibitor, asymmetric kidneys; duplex, CT/MR angiography |
| **Primary aldosteronism** (5–10% of hypertensives, up to 20% of resistant) | **Hypokalaemia (only in about a third),** resistant hypertension, adrenal incidentaloma, sleep apnoea, early-onset hypertension or stroke; **aldosterone:renin ratio** (stop interfering drugs), confirmatory tests, adrenal CT and adrenal vein sampling; surgery (adrenalectomy) or MRA |
| **Phaeochromocytoma/paraganglioma** | **Paroxysmal headache, sweating, palpitations,** labile hypertension, pallor, family history (MEN2, VHL, NF1); **plasma free or 24-hour urinary fractionated metanephrines;** imaging; alpha-blockade before surgery |
| **Cushing syndrome** | Central obesity, striae, bruising, proximal weakness, glucose intolerance; dexamethasone suppression, late-night salivary cortisol |
| **Thyroid disease** | Hyper (systolic) and hypothyroidism (diastolic) |
| **Coarctation of the aorta** | Young patient, radio-femoral delay, arm–leg BP gradient, rib notching; echo, MRI |
| **Sleep apnoea** | Snoring, daytime somnolence, obesity, resistant hypertension, non-dipping pattern |
| **Drugs and substances** | NSAIDs, corticosteroids, oral contraceptives, **decongestants (pseudoephedrine),** liquorice, alcohol excess, cocaine and amphetamines, SNRIs, ciclosporin and tacrolimus, **VEGF inhibitors,** erythropoietin, herbal products |
| **Hyperparathyroidism, acromegaly, pre-eclampsia (pregnancy)** | |

#### Assessment

- **History:** duration, previous readings, symptoms (headache, palpitations, sweating), cardiovascular risk factors, family history, drugs and substances, salt and alcohol intake, sleep, pregnancy history.
- **Examination:** BP in both arms and standing, pulses (radio-femoral delay), cardiac signs (LV heave, S4, murmurs), renal bruits, abdominal masses, fundoscopy (hypertensive retinopathy), BMI and waist, signs of endocrine disease.
- **Baseline tests:** urinalysis and **urine albumin:creatinine ratio,** U&E and eGFR, **potassium** (low suggests aldosteronism), glucose/HbA1c, lipids, FBC, calcium, TSH if indicated, **12-lead ECG** (LVH, AF), **echocardiography** if LVH, cardiac symptoms, or suspected cardiac disease. **Estimate cardiovascular risk** (QRISK, SCORE2, ASCVD calculators).
- **Hypertension-mediated organ damage:** LVH, hypertensive retinopathy (grade III–IV: haemorrhages, exudates, papilloedema), albuminuria, reduced eGFR, carotid plaque, increased pulse-wave velocity, ABI <0.9.

#### Treatment

**Lifestyle:** weight loss, **DASH-type diet, sodium <5–6 g salt/day,** potassium-rich foods (if renal function permits), regular aerobic exercise (150 minutes/week), alcohol reduction, smoking cessation, stress and sleep management.

**Drug therapy:** start drugs in **grade 2–3 hypertension or grade 1 with high cardiovascular risk, diabetes, CKD or organ damage;** in lower-risk grade 1, start after 3–6 months of lifestyle change.

- **First-line classes:** **ACE inhibitors or ARBs; calcium channel blockers (amlodipine); thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics (indapamide, chlortalidone).** Beta-blockers are used for compelling indications (post-MI, heart failure, angina, AF rate control, pregnancy, younger patients with high sympathetic tone).
- **Strategy:** **initial single-pill combination** (ACEi/ARB + CCB or diuretic) in most patients (improves adherence and control); step 2 triple therapy (ACEi/ARB + CCB + thiazide-like); **resistant hypertension** (uncontrolled on three drugs including a diuretic at optimal doses): confirm adherence and measurement (ABPM), exclude secondary causes and interfering drugs, **add spironolactone** (PATHWAY-2), then alpha-blocker, beta-blocker, or others; renal denervation in selected centres.
- **By group:** **Black African/Caribbean patients:** CCB or thiazide-like diuretic first-line (ACEi less effective alone); **diabetes with albuminuria and CKD:** ACEi/ARB; **heart failure:** ACEi/ARB/ARNI, beta-blocker, MRA; **older adults (>80):** start slowly, avoid orthostatic hypotension; **pregnancy:** labetalol, nifedipine, methyldopa (avoid ACEi/ARB).
- **Targets (ESC 2024 / general):** reduce office BP to **<140/90 minimum,** and, if tolerated, **systolic 120–129 mmHg** (and <130/80 in diabetes and CKD per many guidelines); older adults (≥70–80): individualise, targets around 130–139 systolic; avoid diastolic <70 mmHg in coronary disease.
- **Monitor** BP, K⁺, creatinine after starting ACEi/ARB/diuretics; check adherence; annual review.

#### Hypertensive urgency and emergency

- **Hypertensive emergency:** severe hypertension (usually ≥180/120 mmHg) **with acute target organ damage:** hypertensive encephalopathy, **acute stroke (ischaemic or haemorrhagic), aortic dissection, acute coronary syndrome, acute pulmonary oedema, AKI, eclampsia/pre-eclampsia, microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia,** retinal haemorrhages/papilloedema (malignant hypertension), phaeochromocytoma crisis, sympathomimetic toxicity. Needs **ICU/monitored setting and IV titratable drugs.**
- **Principles:** lower BP **by no more than 25% in the first hour,** then to 160/100 over 2–6 hours, then to normal over 24–48 hours (avoid precipitous falls: cerebral and renal hypoperfusion). **Exceptions:** **aortic dissection** (systolic <120 mmHg and heart rate <60 within 20 minutes: IV beta-blocker first (esmolol, labetalol), then vasodilator); **acute ischaemic stroke** (only treat >220/120 unless thrombolysis; Chapter 41); **ICH** (systolic ~130–150); **pre-eclampsia/eclampsia** (labetalol, nifedipine, hydralazine, magnesium); **acute pulmonary oedema** (nitrates, diuretics, CPAP); **phaeochromocytoma** (phentolamine, then beta-blocker); **sympathomimetic toxicity** (benzodiazepines, phentolamine).
- **Drugs:** **labetalol** (IV bolus 20 mg, infusion), **nicardipine, clevidipine, glyceryl trinitrate, sodium nitroprusside (cyanide risk), esmolol, hydralazine** (pregnancy), phentolamine.
- **Hypertensive urgency** (severe asymptomatic hypertension without acute organ damage): **does not require IV therapy or ICU;** reinstate or intensify oral medication, gradual reduction over days; look for non-adherence, pain, anxiety, drugs; avoid sublingual nifedipine.

### 45.2 Coronary artery disease

#### Chronic coronary syndromes (stable angina)

- **Typical angina:** exertional retrosternal pressure relieved by rest or nitrates within minutes (Chapter 22). **Severity:** **CCS class** I (strenuous exertion only) to IV (symptoms at rest).
- **Assess pre-test probability (PTP)** by age, sex and symptom type (ESC 2019 tables: low <5%, 5–15%, 15–85%, >85%): **<5%:** no further testing; **5–15%:** consider testing; **15–85%:** non-invasive imaging; **>85%:** treat as established.
- **Investigations:** resting ECG, echocardiography (LV function, valves, wall motion), **coronary CT angiography (CCTA)** (first-line for low-intermediate PTP and no known CAD; excellent negative predictive value; calcium scoring), **functional imaging** (stress echocardiography, myocardial perfusion SPECT/PET, stress cardiac MRI) for intermediate–high PTP, known CAD, or non-diagnostic CCTA; **exercise ECG** has limited sensitivity; **invasive coronary angiography** with FFR/iFR for high-risk or refractory symptoms.
- **Non-obstructive coronary causes (INOCA/ANOCA):** microvascular angina, vasospastic (Prinzmetal) angina: coronary function testing; treatment with CCB, nitrates, ranolazine, statins.
- **Treatment (all patients):** **lifestyle,** **aspirin 75–100 mg** (or clopidogrel), **statin (high-intensity)** with LDL-C targets (**≥50% reduction and <1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL);** very high risk goals as low as <1.0 mmol/L (40 mg/dL)), add ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors, bempedoic acid/inclisiran as needed, **ACE inhibitor/ARB** (hypertension, diabetes, LV dysfunction, CKD), blood pressure and diabetes control (SGLT2 inhibitors/GLP-1 RAs for diabetes with ASCVD), smoking cessation, **vaccination.** **Antianginal:** **beta-blocker or rate-limiting CCB** first-line; long-acting nitrates, ivabradine, ranolazine, trimetazidine, nicorandil; **sublingual GTN** for acute relief. **Revascularisation** (PCI or CABG) for symptoms refractory to medical therapy, **left main disease, proximal LAD, multivessel disease with diabetes or reduced LVEF (favours CABG; SYNTAX score),** large ischaemic burden; PCI improves symptoms; CABG improves survival in selected groups (ISCHEMIA trial: initial invasive strategy does not reduce hard events in stable CAD without left main).

#### Acute coronary syndromes (ACS)

(Presentation and ECG: Chapter 22.)

- **Definitions:** **unstable angina** (ischaemic symptoms without troponin rise), **NSTEMI** (troponin rise, no persistent ST elevation), **STEMI** (persistent ST elevation or equivalent). **Fourth Universal Definition of MI:** rise/fall of troponin with at least one 99th percentile value plus ischaemic symptoms, ECG changes, imaging evidence, or coronary thrombus; **types 1–5** (type 1 plaque rupture; type 2 supply–demand mismatch (sepsis, anaemia, tachyarrhythmia); type 3 sudden cardiac death; types 4–5 procedure-related).
- **Immediate management:** monitor, IV access, oxygen only if SpO₂ <90%, **aspirin 150–300 mg loading,** **P2Y12 inhibitor** (ticagrelor 180 mg or prasugrel 60 mg preferred; clopidogrel 300–600 mg if anticoagulated or high bleeding risk), **parenteral anticoagulant** (unfractionated heparin, enoxaparin, fondaparinux), analgesia (titrated IV opioid), nitrates if ongoing pain and no hypotension/RV infarct/PDE5 inhibitor use, **beta-blocker** (oral) if no contraindication, **high-intensity statin** early.
- **STEMI:** **primary PCI** (door-to-balloon ≤60–90 minutes; first-medical-contact to wire ≤120 minutes); if PCI cannot be reached within 120 minutes: **fibrinolysis** (tenecteplase, alteplase, reteplase) within 10 minutes of diagnosis (contraindications: prior ICH, ischaemic stroke <6 months, CNS neoplasm, active bleeding, aortic dissection, recent major trauma/surgery) followed by transfer for angiography 2–24 hours later; **rescue PCI** if failed reperfusion (<50% ST resolution at 60–90 minutes). **Cardiogenic shock** (Chapter 38): emergency revascularisation.
- **NSTE-ACS:** risk stratification (GRACE, TIMI); **immediate invasive strategy (<2 hours)** for very high risk (refractory angina, haemodynamic instability, life-threatening arrhythmia, mechanical complication, acute heart failure); **early invasive (<24 hours)** for high risk (rise in troponin, dynamic ST-T changes, GRACE >140); invasive within 72 hours for intermediate-risk; conservative strategy for low risk.
- **Duration of dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT):** **12 months** after ACS (shorter, 1–6 months, in high bleeding risk; individualised), then single antiplatelet; **triple therapy** (DAPT + anticoagulant) minimised in AF: short course then OAC + single antiplatelet.
- **Complications:** **arrhythmias** (VF within 48 hours, AF, bradycardia, AV block in inferior MI), **heart failure and cardiogenic shock,** **mechanical complications** (papillary muscle rupture with acute MR, ventricular septal rupture, free-wall rupture with tamponade: days 3–7), **pericarditis, Dressler syndrome** (weeks), **LV aneurysm and thrombus** (anticoagulate for 3 months), recurrent ischaemia, stroke, **right ventricular infarction** (inferior MI with hypotension and clear lungs: fluids, avoid nitrates).
- **Secondary prevention:** **beta-blocker** (especially LVEF ≤40%, ≥1 year after MI otherwise individualised), **ACEi/ARB/ARNI,** **MRA** (eplerenone/spironolactone) if LVEF ≤40% with heart failure or diabetes, **high-intensity statin** (± ezetimibe/PCSK9 inhibitor) to LDL-C goals, **SGLT2 inhibitor** for diabetes/HF/CKD, **cardiac rehabilitation,** smoking cessation, lifestyle, vaccination, depression screening, return to work and driving advice; **ICD** if LVEF ≤35% >40 days after MI (≥3 months post-revascularisation).
- **MINOCA, SCAD, Takotsubo, coronary vasospasm, cocaine-related ischaemia, myocarditis** (Chapter 22): consider when angiography shows no obstructive disease.

### 45.3 Heart failure

#### Definition and classification

**Heart failure (HF)** is a clinical syndrome of **symptoms (breathlessness, fatigue, ankle swelling) and signs (raised JVP, crackles, oedema, S3) caused by a structural or functional cardiac abnormality** resulting in raised intracardiac pressures and/or inadequate cardiac output at rest or during exercise.

| Type | LVEF | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **HFrEF** (reduced) | ≤40% | Most evidence-based therapy |
| **HFmrEF** (mildly reduced) | 41–49% | Treat like HFrEF in many guidelines (SGLT2i; ARNI/MRA/beta-blocker may help) |
| **HFpEF** (preserved) | ≥50% with raised filling pressures/natriuretic peptides/structural abnormality | Older, women, hypertension, obesity, AF, diabetes, CKD |

**Functional class (NYHA):** I (no limitation), II (slight limitation; ordinary activity causes symptoms), III (marked limitation; less than ordinary activity causes symptoms), IV (symptoms at rest). **ACC/AHA stages:** A (at risk), B (structural disease without symptoms), C (symptomatic), D (advanced/refractory).

#### Diagnosis

1. **Clinical assessment** (symptoms: orthopnoea, PND, exertional dyspnoea, fatigue, oedema, weight gain; signs: raised JVP, hepatojugular reflux, displaced apex, S3, crackles, effusions, hepatomegaly, ascites, peripheral oedema, cachexia).
2. **Natriuretic peptides:** **NT-proBNP >125 pg/mL (BNP >35)** in the non-acute setting, **>300 pg/mL (BNP >100)** in acute presentations make HF likely; **normal levels make HF unlikely** (NPV >95%); raised in AF, renal failure, age, PE, sepsis; **lowered in obesity.**
3. **ECG** (usually abnormal in HFrEF: LBBB, Q waves, LVH, AF), **chest X-ray** (cardiomegaly, pulmonary congestion, effusions), **echocardiography** (essential: LVEF, wall motion, valves, diastolic function (E/e′ >14, LA volume), RV function, pulmonary pressures, pericardium), **blood tests** (U&E, FBC, ferritin and transferrin saturation, glucose, lipids, thyroid function, liver tests, troponin), **cardiac MRI** (aetiology: infarction, myocarditis, infiltration, fibrosis), **coronary assessment** (CT angiography or invasive angiography for ischaemic aetiology), **genetic testing/family screening** in familial cardiomyopathy, **endomyocardial biopsy** in selected cases.

#### Causes

- **Ischaemic heart disease** (most common), **hypertension,** **valvular disease,** **cardiomyopathies** (dilated: idiopathic, familial, alcohol, post-viral/myocarditis, chemotherapy (anthracyclines, trastuzumab), peripartum, tachycardia-induced, thyroid, haemochromatosis, sarcoidosis, Chagas, HIV; hypertrophic; restrictive/infiltrative (amyloid, sarcoid, haemochromatosis); arrhythmogenic RV), **arrhythmias** (AF, frequent PVCs), **congenital heart disease,** **pericardial disease,** **high-output states** (anaemia, thyrotoxicosis, AV fistula, Paget disease, beriberi, sepsis), **pulmonary hypertension/cor pulmonale,** toxins (alcohol, cocaine), endocrine disease.
- **Precipitants of decompensation:** non-adherence (drugs, salt, fluid), **ACS, arrhythmia (AF with fast rate), infection, uncontrolled hypertension, anaemia, renal dysfunction, thyroid disease, PE,** drugs (**NSAIDs, steroids, thiazolidinediones, non-dihydropyridine CCBs, antiarrhythmics, chemotherapy**), alcohol, pregnancy.

#### Management of chronic HFrEF: the four pillars

All four classes reduce mortality and hospitalisation; **start early and in rapid sequence** (often together at low doses within weeks), then **titrate to target doses:**

| Pillar | Drugs (examples) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **1. RAAS inhibition** | **Sacubitril/valsartan (ARNI)** (preferred over ACEi/ARB in symptomatic HFrEF; 36 hours washout from ACEi), ACE inhibitor (ramipril, enalapril, lisinopril) or ARB (candesartan, valsartan) | Monitor K⁺ and creatinine; hypotension, cough (ACEi), angioedema |
| **2. Beta-blocker** | **Bisoprolol, carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, nebivolol** | Start low in stable euvolaemic patients; titrate slowly; avoid in decompensation; reduces mortality and sudden death |
| **3. Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA)** | **Spironolactone, eplerenone** | K⁺ and creatinine at 1 week, 4 weeks; hyperkalaemia, gynaecomastia (spironolactone) |
| **4. SGLT2 inhibitor** | **Dapagliflozin, empagliflozin** | Benefit independent of diabetes; genital mycotic infections, euglycaemic DKA risk |

**Add-ons and other therapies:**

- **Loop diuretics** (furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide) for **congestion** (symptom relief; no mortality benefit); dose to euvolaemia; **thiazide/metolazone** for resistance (caution electrolytes).
- **Ivabradine** (sinus rhythm, HR ≥70 on maximal beta-blocker), **hydralazine + isosorbide dinitrate** (self-identified Black patients with NYHA III–IV on OMT; or ACEi/ARB intolerant), **vericiguat** (worsening HF), **digoxin** (AF rate control, symptomatic despite OMT), **IV iron** (ferric carboxymaltose/derisomaltose) for **iron deficiency** (ferritin <100 µg/L, or 100–299 with TSAT <20%) improves symptoms and reduces hospitalisation, **omega-3** (limited).
- **Avoid:** NSAIDs, non-dihydropyridine CCBs, thiazolidinediones, class I antiarrhythmics, dronedarone, excess salt and fluid (fluid restriction only for hyponatraemia/severe HF).
- **Devices:** **ICD** for primary prevention in **LVEF ≤35%** despite ≥3 months optimal therapy, NYHA II–III, expected survival >1 year (ischaemic: ≥40 days after MI); secondary prevention after VF/VT arrest. **CRT (biventricular pacing)** for **LVEF ≤35% with LBBB and QRS ≥150 ms (class I), QRS 130–149 (IIa)** or non-LBBB with QRS ≥150; **CRT-D** if ICD criteria met. **Conduction system pacing** is an emerging alternative.
- **Revascularisation** for ischaemic cardiomyopathy with viable myocardium; **valve interventions** (functional MR: transcatheter edge-to-edge repair in selected patients; AS: TAVI/SAVR); **AF ablation** in HFrEF (CASTLE-AF); **left ventricular assist devices and transplantation** for advanced HF; **palliative care** in end-stage disease.
- **Self-care and support:** education, daily weights (>2 kg in 3 days: call), salt and fluid advice, exercise training/**cardiac rehabilitation,** vaccination (influenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19), depression screening, remote monitoring, multidisciplinary HF nurse programmes.

#### Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF)

- Diagnose with symptoms/signs, **LVEF ≥50%,** raised natriuretic peptides, and evidence of diastolic dysfunction or structural change (HFA-PEFF/H2FPEF scores); exclude mimics (obesity, lung disease, anaemia, valvular disease, pulmonary hypertension) and specific causes (**cardiac amyloidosis,** HCM, constrictive pericarditis, restrictive cardiomyopathy).
- **Treatment:** **SGLT2 inhibitor** (class I: EMPEROR-Preserved, DELIVER), **diuretics** for congestion, treat hypertension, AF (rhythm control considered), obesity (**GLP-1 receptor agonist semaglutide** or tirzepatide improves symptoms), coronary disease, sleep apnoea, CKD; **MRA, ARNI** may help in selected patients (lower LVEF end); exercise training.

#### Acute heart failure

- **Presentations:** acute decompensated chronic HF (most common), acute pulmonary oedema, cardiogenic shock (Chapter 38), isolated right heart failure, hypertensive acute HF.
- **Management** (parallel): **sit up, oxygen if SpO₂ <90%, CPAP/NIV** for respiratory distress, **IV loop diuretic** (furosemide 40–80 mg IV bolus; or 1–2.5 × home oral dose IV), **vasodilators (IV nitrates)** if SBP >110 mmHg, **treat precipitant** (ACS, arrhythmia, infection, hypertension), **opiates not routinely recommended,** **inotropes** (dobutamine) for hypoperfusion with low output, **vasopressors** for shock, **mechanical support** in refractory cases, **monitor** (weight, urine output, electrolytes, creatinine).
- **Before discharge:** euvolaemic, established on oral GDMT (start or optimise the four pillars in-hospital), education, follow-up within 7 days, HF nurse and rehabilitation.

#### Cardiomyopathies and related conditions

| Condition | Features | Key management points |
|---|---|---|
| **Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)** | Autosomal dominant sarcomere mutations; LV hypertrophy (≥15 mm, no other cause); dynamic outflow obstruction (murmur increased by Valsalva), exertional dyspnoea, chest pain, syncope, **sudden cardiac death (young athletes);** ECG LVH/T-wave inversion; echo/MRI | **Avoid dehydration, vasodilators, high-dose diuretics, digoxin;** beta-blocker, verapamil/diltiazem, disopyramide, **mavacamten** (cardiac myosin inhibitor), septal reduction (myectomy or alcohol septal ablation); **ICD** based on **HCM Risk-SCD** score; **AF anticoagulation regardless of CHA₂DS₂-VASc;** family screening and genetics; competitive sport advice |
| **Dilated cardiomyopathy** | Dilated LV with reduced EF without sufficient coronary disease/valve disease; genetic (TTN, LMNA), alcohol, viral, peripartum | HF therapy; ICD (LMNA high risk); stop toxins; family screening |
| **Arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy** | RV (or biventricular) fibrofatty replacement, VT, syncope, young athletes, epsilon waves | Avoid competitive sport; beta-blocker; ICD; family screening |
| **Restrictive/infiltrative** | **Cardiac amyloidosis** (AL: with myeloma; **ATTR:** older men, carpal tunnel, spinal stenosis, low-voltage ECG with thick walls; technetium scintigraphy, paraprotein tests), sarcoidosis, haemochromatosis | **Tafamidis** (ATTR); chemotherapy (AL); avoid digoxin and non-DHP CCB in amyloid; diuretics |
| **Takotsubo (stress) cardiomyopathy** | Post-emotional/physical stress, women, apical ballooning, ST elevation, troponin rise, normal coronaries | Supportive; ACEi/beta-blocker; recovery in weeks |
| **Peripartum cardiomyopathy** | Last month of pregnancy to 5 months post-partum; HF with LVEF <45% | HF therapy compatible with breastfeeding choices; **bromocriptine** (selected); future pregnancy counselling |
| **Myocarditis** | Preceding viral illness, chest pain, troponin rise, arrhythmia, HF, MRI (oedema, late gadolinium enhancement); immune checkpoint inhibitor myocarditis (fulminant) | Supportive, HF therapy, avoid exercise for 3–6 months, steroids/immunosuppression for giant-cell, sarcoid, eosinophilic and checkpoint-inhibitor myocarditis; mechanical support if fulminant |
| **Pericarditis** | Sharp positional pain, rub, diffuse ST elevation, PR depression | **NSAID (aspirin/ibuprofen) + colchicine** for 3 months, restrict exercise; steroids for refractory; **tamponade** (Beck triad; pericardiocentesis), **constrictive pericarditis** (raised JVP with Kussmaul, pericardial knock; CT/MRI; pericardiectomy) |

### 45.4 Atrial fibrillation (AF) and other arrhythmias

(Diagnosis of palpitations and acute treatment: Chapter 28.)

#### Classification and evaluation

- **Paroxysmal** (terminates within 7 days), **persistent** (>7 days), **long-standing persistent** (>12 months), **permanent** (accepted). Diagnosed by ECG (irregularly irregular, no P waves).
- **Look for causes and risk factors:** hypertension, heart failure, valve disease (mitral stenosis), coronary disease, **thyrotoxicosis, alcohol ("holiday heart"), sleep apnoea, obesity, diabetes, CKD,** acute illness (sepsis, post-operative), PE, pericarditis, congenital heart disease, hypokalaemia, stimulants. Tests: **TSH,** U&E, FBC, ECG, **echocardiography.**
- **Management framework (ESC "AF-CARE"):** **C**omorbidity and risk-factor management, **A**void stroke (anticoagulation), **R**educe symptoms (rate and rhythm control), **E**valuation and dynamic reassessment.

#### Stroke prevention

- **Risk score:** **CHA₂DS₂-VASc** (Congestive HF 1, Hypertension 1, Age ≥75 2, Diabetes 1, Stroke/TIA/embolism 2, Vascular disease 1, Age 65–74 1, Sex category female 1) (ESC 2024 uses **CHA₂DS₂-VA** without sex): **anticoagulate if ≥2 (or ≥3 in women, i.e. ≥2 non-sex factors);** consider at 1 (non-sex).
- **Valvular AF** (moderate–severe mitral stenosis, mechanical prosthetic valve): **warfarin** (target INR 2–3; 2.5–3.5 for mechanical mitral valves), not DOACs.
- **DOACs preferred** (**apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban, dabigatran**) over warfarin; dose reductions by label (e.g. apixaban 2.5 mg twice daily if two of: age ≥80, weight ≤60 kg, creatinine ≥133 µmol/L; rivaroxaban 15 mg with eGFR 15–50; edoxaban 30 mg if eGFR 15–50, weight ≤60, or interacting drugs); avoid inappropriate under-dosing; check renal function.
- **Bleeding risk (HAS-BLED):** identify and **modify** modifiable risks (uncontrolled hypertension, labile INR, alcohol, NSAIDs/antiplatelets, anaemia); a high score **is not a reason to withhold anticoagulation** but to correct risk factors and review closely.
- **Left atrial appendage occlusion** for patients with contraindications to long-term anticoagulation.
- **Cardioversion:** anticoagulate **≥3 weeks before** (or exclude thrombus with transoesophageal echocardiography), and **≥4 weeks after** (long-term if risk factors); **emergency cardioversion** for instability (heparin first).

#### Rate vs rhythm control

- **Rate control** (lenient target resting heart rate <110 bpm if asymptomatic with preserved LV function; stricter if symptomatic): **beta-blockers** (first-line), **diltiazem or verapamil** (not in HFrEF), **digoxin** (sedentary, HF, added to others); **amiodarone** in selected cases; **atrioventricular node ablation with pacemaker** for refractory cases.
- **Rhythm control** (improves symptoms; **early rhythm control** within a year of diagnosis reduces cardiovascular outcomes: EAST-AFNET 4): **electrical or pharmacological cardioversion** (flecainide or propafenone "pill-in-the-pocket" in structurally normal hearts; vernakalant), **antiarrhythmic drugs** (**flecainide/propafenone** if no structural/ischaemic heart disease; **amiodarone** (most effective, most toxic: thyroid, lung, liver, eyes, skin, neuropathy), **sotalol** (QT, renal; caution), **dronedarone** (not in HF), **dofetilide**), **catheter ablation** (pulmonary vein isolation) first-line in selected symptomatic paroxysmal AF and for persistent AF or HFrEF.
- **Acute AF with instability:** DC cardioversion; with stable but fast rate: IV beta-blocker/diltiazem or amiodarone; digoxin or amiodarone in HF.

#### Other arrhythmias in brief

- **Atrial flutter:** regular rate ~150 with 2:1 block; anticoagulate as for AF; **catheter ablation** (cavotricuspid isthmus) highly effective.
- **Sinus node dysfunction and AV block:** **symptomatic bradycardia, Mobitz II, third-degree block:** **permanent pacemaker;** review drugs; **pacing in vasovagal syncope** only selected.
- **Ventricular arrhythmia:** secondary prevention with **ICD;** treat ischaemia, electrolytes, long QT (avoid QT-prolonging drugs; beta-blockers; ICD), **Brugada** (ICD for syncope/arrest), **WPW** (ablation), **premature ventricular contractions** (benign unless >10–15% burden or symptomatic; may cause cardiomyopathy).

### 45.5 Valvular heart disease

#### Aortic stenosis (AS)

- **Causes:** calcific degeneration (most common, older adults), bicuspid valve (younger), rheumatic. **Symptoms:** angina, syncope, exertional dyspnoea, heart failure (average survival 2–3 years after symptom onset without valve replacement). **Signs:** slow-rising pulse, narrow pulse pressure, ejection systolic murmur radiating to the carotids, soft/absent A₂ (Chapter 12).
- **Severity (echo):** **severe AS: peak velocity ≥4.0 m/s, mean gradient ≥40 mmHg, valve area ≤1.0 cm² (indexed ≤0.6 cm²/m²);** low-flow, low-gradient AS (reduced EF or paradoxical) requires CT calcium scoring and dobutamine echo.
- **Treatment:** **valve replacement for symptomatic severe AS** and for asymptomatic severe AS with LVEF <50%, very severe AS, abnormal exercise test, rapidly progressing disease or raised BNP (growing evidence for early intervention); **SAVR vs TAVI** by age (TAVI preferred >75–80 or high surgical risk; SAVR for younger/low-risk by heart team); avoid vasodilators and strenuous exertion in severe AS; no medical therapy alters the course.

#### Aortic regurgitation (AR)

- **Causes:** valve disease (bicuspid, rheumatic, endocarditis), **aortic root disease** (Marfan, hypertension, aortic dissection, syphilis, inflammatory aortitis). **Acute AR** (endocarditis, dissection): severe pulmonary oedema, shock: emergency surgery. **Chronic AR:** collapsing pulse, wide pulse pressure, displaced apex, early diastolic murmur, Austin Flint murmur (Chapter 12).
- **Management:** vasodilators (ACEi, dihydropyridine CCB) for symptoms/hypertension; **surgery** when symptomatic, LVEF ≤55% or LV end-systolic diameter >50 mm (>20 mm/m²), or when undergoing other cardiac surgery; treat aortic dilatation (beta-blockers/ARBs, surgical thresholds ≥55 mm, lower in Marfan/bicuspid).

#### Mitral stenosis (MS)

- **Cause:** **rheumatic** (most common worldwide), calcific in older adults. **Features:** dyspnoea, fatigue, haemoptysis, **AF,** systemic embolism, hoarseness (Ortner), malar flush; **loud S1, opening snap, mid-diastolic rumble;** pulmonary hypertension and RV failure later. **Severity:** valve area **≤1.5 cm² (clinically significant),** mean gradient >10 mmHg (heart rate-dependent).
- **Management:** heart-rate control (beta-blocker, to lengthen diastole), diuretics, **anticoagulation with warfarin (not DOAC) for AF, embolism or LA thrombus;** **percutaneous mitral balloon commissurotomy** (preferred for pliable valves without significant MR/LA thrombus) or surgery for symptomatic significant stenosis; **secondary prophylaxis** against rheumatic fever (penicillin).

#### Mitral regurgitation (MR)

- **Primary (degenerative: prolapse, flail leaflet; rheumatic; endocarditis; ischaemic papillary rupture)** vs **secondary (functional: dilated LV or ischaemia).** Acute severe MR (papillary rupture, endocarditis, chordal rupture): pulmonary oedema, shock; emergency surgery. **Chronic:** pansystolic murmur radiating to the axilla, displaced apex, S3, AF.
- **Management:** **surgery (repair preferred) for symptomatic severe primary MR,** or asymptomatic with LVEF ≤60%, LVESD ≥40 mm, AF, pulmonary hypertension; **transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (MitraClip)** for selected high-risk patients and for secondary MR in HF on optimal therapy (COAPT); treat HF (secondary MR).

#### Tricuspid and pulmonary valve disease

- **Tricuspid regurgitation:** usually functional (RV dilatation, pulmonary hypertension, AF); or endocarditis (IV drug use), carcinoid, Ebstein anomaly, device leads; **raised JVP with large v waves,** hepatomegaly, ascites, oedema; diuretics; surgery/transcatheter therapy in severe symptomatic cases.
- **Pulmonary stenosis:** congenital; balloon valvuloplasty.

#### Infective endocarditis (IE)

- **Risk:** prosthetic valves, previous IE, congenital heart disease, rheumatic and degenerative valve disease, **IV drug use,** indwelling devices/lines, haemodialysis, poor dentition, immunosuppression. **Organisms:** *Staphylococcus aureus* (most common in many regions; aggressive), **viridans streptococci** (subacute, dental), enterococci, HACEK group, coagulase-negative staphylococci (prosthetic valves), *Candida*; culture-negative (prior antibiotics, *Coxiella, Bartonella,* *Brucella,* *Tropheryma whipplei*).
- **Presentation:** fever, **new or changing murmur,** malaise, weight loss, night sweats, embolic phenomena (stroke, splenic and renal infarcts, septic emboli to lungs in right-sided IE), **immunological** (Osler nodes, Roth spots, glomerulonephritis), **peripheral stigmata** (splinter haemorrhages, Janeway lesions), heart failure, conduction abnormalities (aortic root abscess: new PR prolongation).
- **Diagnosis (modified Duke/2023 ESC criteria):** **blood cultures ×3 sets (≥1 hour apart, before antibiotics)** (typically positive in 90%), **echocardiography** (transthoracic then **transoesophageal** if TTE negative with high suspicion, prosthetic valve or complications), **PET-CT or cardiac CT** for prosthetic valve/device infection and complications, **brain imaging** for emboli; inflammatory markers; major criteria: typical organisms in two cultures, endocardial involvement (vegetation, abscess, new dehiscence, new regurgitation).
- **Treatment:** **prolonged IV bactericidal antibiotics (4–6 weeks)** tailored to organism and susceptibility (e.g. benzylpenicillin or amoxicillin ± gentamicin for streptococci; flucloxacillin for MSSA; vancomycin/daptomycin for MRSA; prosthetic valve: add rifampicin); **multidisciplinary Endocarditis Team;** **early surgery** for **heart failure from valve dysfunction, uncontrolled infection (abscess, fungal, persistent bacteraemia >5–7 days), or high embolic risk** (vegetations >10 mm with embolic events, >30 mm); partial oral step-down in selected stable patients (POET); treat addiction; dental review.
- **Antibiotic prophylaxis before dental procedures** only for **highest-risk patients** (prosthetic valve or material used for valve repair, **previous IE,** certain congenital heart disease): amoxicillin 2 g oral 30–60 minutes before; maintain dental hygiene.

#### Prosthetic valves and anticoagulation

- **Mechanical valves:** **lifelong warfarin** (INR target 2.5 for bileaflet aortic valve without risk factors; **3.0 for mitral position or aortic with risk factors (AF, previous thromboembolism, LV dysfunction, hypercoagulable state));** **DOACs are contraindicated;** aspirin addition only in selected high-risk; pregnancy requires specialist management (warfarin teratogenic in first trimester; LMWH).
- **Bioprosthetic valves:** warfarin (INR 2–3) or aspirin for 3–6 months, then aspirin long-term (or no antithrombotic if no risk factors); anticoagulation for AF.
- **Surveillance:** baseline echo, symptoms, infection vigilance, structural valve deterioration (TAVI follow-up).

#### Rheumatic fever and rheumatic heart disease

- **Acute rheumatic fever (ARF):** 2–3 weeks after group A streptococcal pharyngitis (children 5–15 years); **revised Jones criteria (2015):** **evidence of preceding GAS infection** (ASO titre, throat culture) plus **2 major** (carditis, polyarthritis (low-risk populations) or polyarthralgia (high-risk), chorea, erythema marginatum, subcutaneous nodules) or **1 major + 2 minor** (fever ≥38.5 °C (≥38 in high-risk), arthralgia, ESR ≥60/CRP ≥3 mg/dL, prolonged PR). **Treatment:** penicillin to eradicate GAS, **aspirin/NSAIDs** for arthritis, rest, steroids for severe carditis, **secondary prophylaxis** (benzathine penicillin G every 3–4 weeks for at least 10 years or until age 21–40 depending on carditis and risk); **echocardiographic screening** in endemic regions.

### 45.6 Venous thromboembolism (VTE)

#### Overview

**Deep vein thrombosis (DVT)** and **pulmonary embolism (PE)** are manifestations of the same disease. **Risk factors (Virchow's triad):** **stasis** (immobility, long travel, paralysis, heart failure, obesity), **hypercoagulability** (cancer, pregnancy/puerperium, oestrogen (combined contraceptive, HRT), inherited thrombophilia (factor V Leiden, prothrombin G20210A, protein C/S and antithrombin deficiency), antiphospholipid syndrome, nephrotic syndrome, inflammatory bowel disease, myeloproliferative neoplasm, PNH, infection/COVID-19, heparin-induced thrombocytopenia), **endothelial injury** (surgery, trauma, central lines). **Provoked** (major transient risk factor within 3 months: surgery with anaesthesia >30 minutes, hospitalisation ≥3 days, trauma/fracture, bedbound), **minor transient, or unprovoked.**

#### Diagnosis (Chapter 70)

- **Clinical probability** (Wells score for DVT and PE; Geneva; **PERC** rule to exclude PE in low-risk patients), **D-dimer** (age-adjusted threshold for patients >50) if probability low or intermediate, **compression ultrasound** for suspected DVT, **CTPA** (or V/Q scan if contrast contraindicated or pregnant) for PE; **ECG** (sinus tachycardia; S1Q3T3 rare; RV strain), **troponin, BNP, echocardiography** (RV dilatation, McConnell sign) for risk stratification; consider **lower limb ultrasound** in PE.
- **Pregnancy:** compression ultrasound first; chest X-ray, V/Q or low-dose CTPA; avoid D-dimer as a rule-out in late pregnancy (specificity low).

#### Treatment

- **Start anticoagulation immediately if high or intermediate probability** while awaiting tests.
- **Initial and maintenance options:**
  - **DOACs (first-line in most patients):** **apixaban 10 mg twice daily for 7 days then 5 mg twice daily;** **rivaroxaban 15 mg twice daily for 21 days then 20 mg once daily with food;** **edoxaban 60 mg daily** and **dabigatran 150 mg twice daily** after ≥5 days of parenteral anticoagulation.
  - **LMWH** (enoxaparin 1.5 mg/kg daily or 1 mg/kg twice daily; dalteparin) for pregnancy, **cancer-associated VTE (or apixaban/edoxaban/rivaroxaban if low GI/GU bleeding risk),** severe renal failure (UFH), initial therapy before warfarin or dabigatran/edoxaban; **fondaparinux.**
  - **Warfarin** (target INR 2–3) overlapping with LMWH for ≥5 days and until INR ≥2 for 24 hours; **antiphospholipid syndrome (triple positive):** warfarin preferred over DOACs; **mechanical valves, severe renal impairment, morbid obesity (>120 kg), extreme weights:** specialist advice.
- **Duration:**
  - **Provoked by major transient risk factor:** **3 months.**
  - **Unprovoked or persistent risk factor (including cancer):** **at least 3–6 months, then consider extended anticoagulation** (indefinite with periodic review) unless high bleeding risk; reduced-dose apixaban (2.5 mg bd) or rivaroxaban (10 mg daily) after 6 months for extended prophylaxis; **cancer:** continue while cancer is active.
  - **Distal (calf) DVT:** 3 months if symptomatic or risk factors; surveillance ultrasound in low-risk.
- **Thrombolysis (alteplase) and catheter-directed therapy** for **high-risk PE** (haemodynamic instability: sustained SBP <90 mmHg, vasopressors, cardiac arrest) and rescue for deterioration on anticoagulation; **surgical embolectomy** if contraindicated; **ECMO.** **Intermediate-high-risk PE** (RV dysfunction on imaging + raised troponin) needs **monitoring and anticoagulation** (PEITHO: routine thrombolysis not recommended; rescue if decompensation).
- **IVC filter** only if anticoagulation is absolutely contraindicated or recurrent VTE despite therapeutic anticoagulation.
- **Severity classification (ESC):** high risk (haemodynamic instability), intermediate-high (RV dysfunction + troponin), intermediate-low (one of them), **low risk** (sPESI 0, no RV dysfunction): consider **outpatient treatment.**
- **Extensive iliofemoral DVT** with limb threat (phlegmasia): catheter-directed thrombolysis, thrombectomy.
- **Compression stockings** for symptomatic relief only (not routine prevention of post-thrombotic syndrome).
- **Complications and follow-up:** **recurrent VTE,** **post-thrombotic syndrome,** **post-PE syndrome (persistent dyspnoea),** **chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH)** (V/Q scan if persistent breathlessness after 3 months; treated with PEA surgery, balloon pulmonary angioplasty, riociguat), bleeding on anticoagulation.
- **Thrombophilia testing:** not routine; consider in young patients (<50) with unprovoked VTE and strong family history, unusual sites, or recurrent events, where results would change management; **do not test during acute thrombosis or on anticoagulants** (false results).
- **Unprovoked VTE and cancer screening:** history, examination, basic tests, and age-appropriate screening; extensive CT screening is not routinely recommended.
- **Prophylaxis:** inpatient risk assessment, LMWH/mechanical prophylaxis; extended prophylaxis after major orthopaedic and cancer surgery; early mobilisation; travel advice.
- **Superficial vein thrombosis:** ≥5 cm or near the saphenofemoral junction: prophylactic dose fondaparinux or LMWH for 45 days.

### 45.7 Other cardiovascular conditions

#### Peripheral arterial disease (PAD)

- **Symptoms:** **intermittent claudication** (reproducible calf/thigh/buttock pain on walking, relieved by rest within minutes), **chronic limb-threatening ischaemia (CLTI)** (rest pain, non-healing ulcers, gangrene), **acute limb ischaemia** (6 Ps: Pain, Pallor, Pulselessness, Paraesthesia, Paralysis, Perishing cold; emergency: heparin, urgent revascularisation).
- **Diagnosis:** **ankle–brachial pressure index (ABPI):** normal 1.0–1.4; **<0.9 diagnostic;** <0.5 severe; **>1.4** non-compressible (diabetes, CKD: use toe pressure/TBI); duplex ultrasound, CT/MR angiography.
- **Management:** **supervised exercise therapy,** smoking cessation, **statin, antiplatelet (clopidogrel or aspirin; low-dose rivaroxaban 2.5 mg bd + aspirin in selected),** blood pressure and diabetes control, foot care, **revascularisation** (endovascular or surgical) for lifestyle-limiting claudication refractory to therapy and for CLTI (urgent); amputation prevention.
- PAD indicates **generalised atherosclerosis:** high risk of MI and stroke.

#### Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA)

- **Definition:** aortic diameter ≥3.0 cm. **Risk:** male, age >65, smoking, family history, hypertension. **Screening:** **one-time ultrasound in men at 65 (UK/US)** reduces AAA mortality. **Surveillance:** 3.0–4.4 cm every 3 years; 4.5–5.4 cm annually (or every 3–6 months); **repair at ≥5.5 cm in men (≥5.0 cm in women), or if growth >1 cm/year, or symptomatic;** **EVAR** vs open repair by anatomy and fitness. **Rupture:** emergency (Chapter 25, 42).
- **Thoracic aortic aneurysm and dissection:** connective tissue disease (Marfan, Ehlers–Danlos vascular type, Loeys–Dietz), bicuspid aortic valve, hypertension; imaging surveillance and repair thresholds (≥5.5 cm; lower in genetic syndromes); **acute aortic syndromes** (Chapter 22).

#### Carotid stenosis

- Symptomatic stenosis 70–99% (or 50–69% in selected): **carotid endarterectomy within 2 weeks** (Chapter 41); asymptomatic: best medical therapy; revascularisation selectively.

#### Pulmonary hypertension (PH)

- **Definition (2022 ESC/ERS):** **mean pulmonary artery pressure >20 mmHg** at rest on right heart catheterisation; pre-capillary PH: PVR >2 Wood units, PAWP ≤15 mmHg.
- **Groups:** **1** PAH (idiopathic, heritable, connective tissue disease (systemic sclerosis), HIV, portal hypertension, congenital heart disease, drugs); **2** left heart disease (commonest); **3** lung disease/hypoxia (COPD, ILD, sleep apnoea); **4** chronic thromboembolic (CTEPH); **5** multifactorial/others (sarcoidosis, haematological).
- **Presentation:** exertional dyspnoea, fatigue, syncope, chest pain, signs of RV failure (raised JVP, loud P₂, RV heave, TR murmur, oedema); echo (estimated PASP, RV dilatation), then V/Q scan (CTEPH), PFTs, CT, **right heart catheterisation** (diagnostic standard). **Treatment:** PAH-specific therapy (endothelin receptor antagonists, PDE5 inhibitors, riociguat, prostacyclins, selexipag, sotatercept), anticoagulation in selected, diuretics, oxygen, **referral to a PH centre;** treat the underlying cause in other groups.

#### Congenital heart disease in adults (brief)

- **ASD** (fixed split S2, dyspnoea, AF, paradoxical embolism; closure if significant shunt); **VSD** (pansystolic murmur; Eisenmenger syndrome if untreated: cyanosis, clubbing); **coarctation** (hypertension, radio-femoral delay; repair/stent); **bicuspid aortic valve;** **tetralogy of Fallot** (post-repair: pulmonary regurgitation, arrhythmias); **Ebstein anomaly;** **transposition** (post-switch). Lifelong specialist follow-up, endocarditis vigilance, contraception/pregnancy counselling.

#### Cardiac tumours

- **Atrial myxoma** (left atrium, women 30–60: dyspnoea, syncope, embolism, constitutional symptoms; echo; surgical excision). Metastases are more common than primary cardiac tumours.

### 45.8 Worked cases

#### Case 1: Resistant hypertension with hypokalaemia

*A 44-year-old woman has BP 168/104 mmHg on perindopril 8 mg, amlodipine 10 mg and indapamide 2.5 mg, adherent (ABPM confirms). K⁺ 3.0 mmol/L despite no diarrhoea or vomiting. She snores. Aldosterone:renin ratio high; saline suppression confirms; CT: 1.4-cm left adrenal adenoma; adrenal vein sampling lateralises left.*

**Reasoning.** **Primary aldosteronism (Conn adenoma)** causing resistant hypertension and hypokalaemia.

**Action.** **Laparoscopic left adrenalectomy** (potentially curative); if surgery is not feasible, **spironolactone or eplerenone;** screen for sleep apnoea, cardiovascular risk, replace potassium; post-operative monitoring (hypoaldosteronism, hyperkalaemia).

#### Case 2: Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction

*A 66-year-old man with a previous anterior MI has 3 months of exertional dyspnoea, orthopnoea and ankle swelling. BP 108/68, HR 88 sinus, JVP raised, S3, bibasal crackles, pitting oedema. NT-proBNP 4,200. Echo: LVEF 28%, dilated LV, anterior akinesis. eGFR 62, K⁺ 4.4. Ferritin 70, TSAT 14%.*

**Reasoning.** **Ischaemic HFrEF, NYHA III, with iron deficiency.**

**Action.** Loop diuretic for congestion; start **sacubitril/valsartan, bisoprolol, spironolactone and dapagliflozin** (low dose, up-titrate over weeks, monitoring K⁺ and creatinine), **IV iron** (ferric carboxymaltose), coronary assessment/revascularisation if viable, **ICD ± CRT** if LVEF remains ≤35% after 3 months of optimal therapy (QRS width), cardiac rehabilitation, education and follow-up.

#### Case 3: Atrial fibrillation: anticoagulation decision

*A 74-year-old woman with hypertension and type 2 diabetes has irregular palpitations for 3 weeks; ECG: AF at 98 bpm; TSH normal; echo: LA enlargement, normal LVEF. CHA₂DS₂-VASc: age 65–74 (1) + hypertension (1) + diabetes (1) + female (1) = 4.*

**Reasoning.** **Persistent AF with high stroke risk.**

**Action.** **DOAC (apixaban 5 mg twice daily; creatinine and weight check),** rate control with bisoprolol (target <110 bpm), consider **early rhythm control** (cardioversion after ≥3 weeks anticoagulation or with TOE; ablation if symptomatic), treat hypertension/diabetes, reduce alcohol, screen for sleep apnoea; HAS-BLED modifiable factors.

#### Case 4: Severe aortic stenosis

*A 79-year-old man has exertional chest tightness and one episode of syncope. Slow-rising pulse, ejection systolic murmur grade 4/6 radiating to the carotids, soft A₂. Echo: peak velocity 4.6 m/s, mean gradient 52 mmHg, valve area 0.7 cm², LVEF 55%. Coronary angiography: no significant disease.*

**Reasoning.** **Severe symptomatic calcific aortic stenosis.**

**Action.** Heart team evaluation: **TAVI** (age, comorbidity) or SAVR; avoid vasodilators and heavy exertion in the interim; dental review; endocarditis counselling; post-procedure antithrombotic therapy and follow-up.

#### Case 5: Pulmonary embolism: risk stratification

*A 58-year-old man, 2 weeks after knee surgery, has sudden pleuritic chest pain and dyspnoea. HR 112, BP 118/72, SpO₂ 92% on air, RR 24. D-dimer very high. CTPA: bilateral lobar PE, RV:LV ratio 1.3. Troponin raised. Wells score 6.*

**Reasoning.** **Intermediate-high-risk PE** (haemodynamically stable with RV dysfunction and raised troponin).

**Action.** Oxygen, **immediate anticoagulation** (LMWH or apixaban per local protocol; avoid delay), admit to a monitored bed, close observation for deterioration (rescue thrombolysis if hypotension), echocardiography, **provoked VTE: anticoagulate for 3 months,** early mobilisation, follow-up for post-PE syndrome and CTEPH if persistent dyspnoea.

#### Case 6: Infective endocarditis

*A 36-year-old man who injects drugs has 3 weeks of fever, night sweats, and a new pansystolic murmur at the left lower sternal edge, with cough and pleuritic pain. Blood cultures: *S. aureus* in 3/3 sets. Echo: 12-mm tricuspid vegetation; chest CT: septic pulmonary emboli.*

**Reasoning.** **Right-sided *S. aureus* endocarditis (tricuspid) with septic emboli.**

**Action.** Endocarditis team, IV anti-staphylococcal therapy (flucloxacillin for MSSA; vancomycin if MRSA) for 2–6 weeks (shorter regimens possible for uncomplicated right-sided MSSA), repeat blood cultures, surgery only if heart failure, persistent infection or large vegetations with recurrent emboli; addiction treatment (opioid agonist therapy), harm reduction, HIV/hepatitis screening.

#### Case 7: Acute coronary syndrome pathway

*A 61-year-old man with 40 minutes of crushing chest pain: ECG shows 4 mm ST elevation in V1–V4; HR 94, BP 138/84, no heart failure. The nearest PCI centre is 40 minutes away.*

**Reasoning.** **Anterior STEMI.**

**Action.** Aspirin 300 mg, P2Y12 inhibitor, heparin, analgesia, **activate primary PCI** (target first-medical-contact-to-wire <120 minutes); post-PCI: DAPT 12 months, high-intensity statin, beta-blocker/ACEi (if LVEF ≤40%), cardiac rehab, smoking cessation, echo for LVEF, ICD reassessment at 40 days if LVEF ≤35%.

### 45.9 Common pitfalls

- **Diagnosing hypertension on a single reading** or with incorrect cuff size; failing to use ABPM/HBPM; treating "white-coat" hypertension.
- **Missing secondary hypertension** (aldosteronism, renal disease, sleep apnoea, drugs) in resistant or early-onset hypertension.
- **Lowering BP rapidly in non-emergencies;** using sublingual nifedipine; failing to lower BP in aortic dissection or ICH.
- **Over-reliance on exercise ECG** for stable chest pain; under-use of CCTA; failing to optimise secondary prevention (statin dose, LDL targets).
- **Delaying reperfusion** in STEMI; giving unnecessary oxygen; omitting the P2Y12 inhibitor or anticoagulant.
- **Starting HFrEF therapy slowly and sequentially** (missing the four pillars); using NSAIDs, non-dihydropyridine CCBs; withholding beta-blockers in stable HF; ignoring iron deficiency.
- **Treating HFpEF only with diuretics** (omitting SGLT2 inhibitors and comorbidity management); missing amyloid.
- **Withholding anticoagulation in AF because of HAS-BLED or age;** using aspirin instead of OAC; under-dosing DOACs; using DOACs in mechanical valves or moderate–severe mitral stenosis.
- **Rate-control drugs (verapamil/diltiazem) in HFrEF;** AV-nodal blockers in pre-excited AF.
- **Missing endocarditis** (not taking three blood-culture sets before antibiotics; attributing fever to other causes).
- **Delaying valve intervention** until irreversible LV damage in severe symptomatic AS or MR.
- **Over-testing for thrombophilia;** stopping anticoagulation too early in unprovoked VTE; missing cancer-associated VTE needs.
- **Missing PAD by examination alone** (ABPI), and neglecting systemic risk in PAD.

---

### 45.10 Key points

- Confirm hypertension with ABPM/HBPM; screen for secondary causes in early-onset, resistant, or severe cases; use single-pill combinations (ACEi/ARB + CCB or thiazide-like); hypertensive emergency means acute organ damage and needs IV titratable therapy with controlled reduction.
- Stable CAD: PTP-based testing (CCTA or functional imaging), antiplatelet, high-intensity statin, antianginals, revascularisation for refractory symptoms or prognostic lesions; ACS: rapid antithrombotic therapy and revascularisation (primary PCI ≤120 minutes), 12 months of DAPT, comprehensive secondary prevention.
- HF: diagnose with NT-proBNP and echo; HFrEF: four pillars (ARNI/ACEi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i), diuretics for congestion, iron, devices (ICD/CRT); HFpEF: SGLT2i, diuretics, comorbidity control.
- AF: assess CHA₂DS₂-VASc; DOAC for most (warfarin for mechanical valves/moderate–severe MS); early rhythm control and ablation for symptomatic patients.
- Valve disease: AS treated by TAVI/SAVR when symptomatic; MS by balloon valvotomy; endocarditis needs cultures, echo, prolonged antibiotics and timely surgery; prophylaxis only for the highest risk.
- VTE: risk-stratify PE, treat with DOACs, tailor duration to provoking factors, avoid unnecessary thrombophilia testing.
- Recognise cardiomyopathies, myocarditis, pericardial disease, pulmonary hypertension, PAD, and aortic aneurysm.

### 45.11 Self-assessment

1. State the ambulatory and home BP thresholds for hypertension.
   *Answer: ABPM daytime ≥135/85 (24-hour ≥130/80), home BP ≥135/85 mmHg.*

2. List the four pillars of therapy in HFrEF.
   *Answer: ARNI (or ACEi/ARB), evidence-based beta-blocker, MRA, and SGLT2 inhibitor (plus diuretics for congestion).*

3. Calculate the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score for a 78-year-old man with heart failure and hypertension, and say whether to anticoagulate.
   *Answer: Age ≥75 (2) + HF (1) + hypertension (1) = 4; anticoagulate (DOAC).*

4. What are the criteria for severe aortic stenosis and the indication for intervention?
   *Answer: Peak velocity ≥4 m/s, mean gradient ≥40 mmHg, valve area ≤1.0 cm²; intervention (TAVI/SAVR) for symptomatic severe AS, and in selected asymptomatic patients (LVEF <50%, very severe AS, abnormal exercise test).*

5. Which anticoagulant and INR target are used for a mechanical mitral valve?
   *Answer: Warfarin, INR target about 3.0 (2.5–3.5); DOACs are contraindicated.*

6. How long should anticoagulation continue after a first unprovoked PE?
   *Answer: At least 3–6 months; then consider extended (indefinite) anticoagulation, with periodic review of bleeding risk, using reduced-dose DOAC if appropriate.*
