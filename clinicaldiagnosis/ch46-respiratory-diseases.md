## Chapter 46. Respiratory Diseases

### Learning objectives

After studying this chapter you should be able to:

1. Diagnose community-acquired, hospital-acquired and aspiration pneumonia, assess severity (CURB-65), choose empirical antibiotics, and recognise complications and failure to respond.
2. Diagnose asthma using variable symptoms and objective variable airflow limitation, and apply stepwise treatment based on anti-inflammatory therapy (GINA).
3. Diagnose COPD with spirometry, classify severity and exacerbation risk, and apply smoking cessation, inhaled therapy, rehabilitation, oxygen and non-invasive ventilation criteria.
4. Diagnose tuberculosis (active and latent), interpret microbiological tests, and outline treatment, drug resistance, and public-health measures.
5. Evaluate pleural effusion (Light's criteria, pleural fluid analysis) and pneumothorax, and know their management.
6. Recognise interstitial lung disease, sarcoidosis, bronchiectasis, lung cancer, pulmonary nodules, and obstructive sleep apnoea.
7. Integrate history, examination, spirometry, imaging and microbiology to reach a diagnosis and plan.

---

### 46.1 Approach to respiratory disease

Respiratory diagnoses rest on **five pillars:**

1. **Symptoms** (cough, sputum, haemoptysis, dyspnoea, wheeze, chest pain; Chapter 23) with **time course** and **exposures** (smoking in pack-years, occupation, pets, travel, TB contacts, drugs).
2. **Examination** (Chapter 13).
3. **Physiology:** pulse oximetry (including exertional), **spirometry** (obstructive: FEV₁/FVC <0.70; restrictive: reduced FVC with normal ratio), lung volumes, **gas transfer (DLCO),** peak flow, arterial blood gas.
4. **Imaging:** chest X-ray, **CT/HRCT,** ultrasound, PET-CT, V/Q.
5. **Microbiology and tissue:** sputum, bronchoscopy with lavage and biopsy, pleural fluid, lung biopsy.

**Pattern recognition:** obstructive (asthma, COPD, bronchiectasis, bronchiolitis), restrictive (ILD, chest wall, neuromuscular, pleural), infective (pneumonia, TB, abscess), vascular (PE, pulmonary hypertension), neoplastic (lung cancer, mesothelioma), and sleep-disordered breathing.

### 46.2 Pneumonia

#### Definitions and classification

- **Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP):** acute lower respiratory tract infection with new radiographic infiltrate, acquired outside hospital (or within 48 hours of admission).
- **Hospital-acquired pneumonia (HAP):** onset ≥48 hours after admission; **ventilator-associated (VAP)** ≥48 hours after intubation.
- **Aspiration pneumonia/pneumonitis:** inhalation of oropharyngeal or gastric contents (stroke, dysphagia, reduced consciousness, alcohol, seizures, dementia, GORD).
- **Pneumonia in the immunocompromised** (neutropenia, transplant, HIV, steroids).

#### Aetiology (CAP)

| Organism | Features |
|---|---|
| ***Streptococcus pneumoniae*** | Most common; abrupt onset, rigors, pleuritic pain, rusty sputum, lobar consolidation, high fever, leucocytosis |
| ***Mycoplasma pneumoniae*** | Young adults, gradual onset, dry cough, headache, **extrapulmonary** features (haemolytic anaemia with cold agglutinins, rash, erythema multiforme, Guillain–Barré), epidemics every 3–5 years |
| ***Legionella pneumophila*** | Contaminated water systems (hotels, cruise ships, air-conditioning); **confusion, diarrhoea, hyponatraemia, raised CK/LFTs, high fever;** often severe |
| ***Haemophilus influenzae*, *Moraxella catarrhalis*** | COPD |
| ***Staphylococcus aureus*** | Post-influenza, IV drug use, necrotising/cavitating (Panton–Valentine leucocidin) |
| **Gram-negatives** (*Klebsiella,* *E. coli,* *Pseudomonas*) | Alcohol misuse, nursing-home residents, bronchiectasis, recent hospitalisation |
| **Viruses** | Influenza, SARS-CoV-2, RSV, adenovirus, metapneumovirus |
| ***Chlamydophila psittaci*** | Bird exposure |
| ***Coxiella burnetii*** (Q fever) | Farm animals, abattoirs |
| **Anaerobes** | Aspiration; lung abscess, empyema |
| ***Pneumocystis jirovecii*** | HIV, steroids, transplant |
| ***Mycobacterium tuberculosis*** | Subacute |

#### Clinical features and diagnosis

- **Symptoms:** cough (productive or dry), fever, dyspnoea, pleuritic pain, malaise; **older adults:** confusion, falls, reduced appetite, without fever or cough.
- **Signs:** tachypnoea, tachycardia, fever, focal crackles, bronchial breathing, dullness, increased vocal resonance, pleural rub; hypoxia.
- **Diagnosis:** **clinical features plus chest X-ray** (consolidation, air bronchograms; may be normal early or dehydrated; CT is more sensitive).
- **Investigations (by severity):** oximetry/ABG, **FBC, U&E (urea), CRP** (procalcitonin as adjunct), liver tests; **blood cultures** (moderate–severe), **sputum Gram stain and culture,** **urinary antigens** (pneumococcus, *Legionella* serogroup 1), **respiratory PCR panel** (influenza, SARS-CoV-2, atypicals), HIV test in selected patients, **pleural fluid sampling** if effusion.
- **Severity: CURB-65 (one point each):** **C**onfusion (new), **U**rea >7 mmol/L (BUN >19 mg/dL), **R**espiratory rate ≥30, **B**lood pressure (systolic <90 or diastolic ≤60), age **≥65.** **Score 0–1:** low risk (home treatment); **2:** consider hospital; **≥3:** severe (high mortality; consider ICU if 4–5). **PSI/PORT** and **ATS/IDSA severe CAP criteria** (**major:** septic shock requiring vasopressors, respiratory failure requiring mechanical ventilation; **minor (≥3):** RR ≥30, PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤250, multilobar infiltrates, confusion, urea ≥7, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, hypothermia, hypotension requiring fluids) guide ICU admission. Scores support judgement; also consider social circumstances, comorbidity, hypoxia, and inability to take oral therapy.

#### Treatment (follow local antibiogram and guidelines)

| Severity | Typical empirical therapy | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Low (CURB 0–1)** | **Amoxicillin 500 mg–1 g three times daily** (or doxycycline or a macrolide if penicillin-allergic) | **5 days** (extend if not clinically stable) |
| **Moderate (CURB 2)** | **Amoxicillin plus clarithromycin or doxycycline** (or a respiratory fluoroquinolone: levofloxacin/moxifloxacin) | 5–7 days |
| **Severe (CURB ≥3, ICU)** | **IV beta-lactam (co-amoxiclav, ceftriaxone or cefotaxime) plus macrolide (clarithromycin/azithromycin)** (± fluoroquinolone if *Legionella* suspected); add anti-MRSA (vancomycin/linezolid) and anti-pseudomonal cover if risk factors | 7–10 days (*Legionella* 14–21) |
| **Aspiration** | Covered by standard CAP regimens; add anaerobic cover for lung abscess/empyema (amoxicillin-clavulanate, clindamycin, metronidazole) | |
| **HAP/VAP** | **Anti-pseudomonal beta-lactam** (piperacillin-tazobactam, cefepime, meropenem) **± MRSA cover** (vancomycin or linezolid) per risk of resistance | 7 days |

- **Supportive:** oxygen (SpO₂ 94–98%; 88–92% in hypercapnic risk), fluids, analgesia (paracetamol, NSAID for pleuritic pain), VTE prophylaxis, early mobilisation, nutritional support, treat comorbidity.
- **Corticosteroids:** **hydrocortisone 200 mg/day** reduces mortality in **severe CAP** (CAPE COD) and may help in some viral pneumonias (COVID-19: dexamethasone 6 mg); not for routine non-severe CAP.
- **Influenza:** **oseltamivir** (within 48 hours or in severe/hospitalised patients regardless of time).
- **Reassess at 48–72 hours** (clinical improvement, fever resolution, CRP trend); **switch to oral** when stable (temperature ≤37.8 °C, HR ≤100, RR ≤24, SBP ≥90, SpO₂ ≥90%, able to eat).
- **Failure to respond (no improvement by 72 hours):** reconsider diagnosis (**PE, heart failure, lung cancer with post-obstructive pneumonia, organising pneumonia, vasculitis, eosinophilic pneumonia, drug reaction**), resistant or unusual organism (*S. aureus,* *Legionella,* TB, fungi, *Pneumocystis*), **complications** (parapneumonic effusion/empyema, lung abscess, ARDS, sepsis, endocarditis, metastatic infection), immunosuppression, inadequate dosing or adherence.
- **Complications:**
  - **Parapneumonic effusion and empyema:** diagnostic aspiration if effusion >10 mm on ultrasound: **pH <7.2, glucose <3.3 mmol/L, LDH >1,000 U/L, positive culture or pus = complicated/empyema → chest drain** plus antibiotics ± **intrapleural tPA + DNase** (MIST-2); surgery (VATS decortication) if failed.
  - **Lung abscess** (aspiration, *S. aureus,* anaerobes, *Klebsiella*): prolonged antibiotics (weeks), drainage rarely.
  - **Septic shock, ARDS, respiratory failure,** acute kidney injury, arrhythmia and myocardial infarction (increased cardiovascular risk after pneumonia), delirium.
- **Follow-up:** clinical review at 6 weeks; **repeat chest X-ray at 6 weeks in patients >50 years, smokers, or with persisting symptoms** (exclude underlying lung cancer); smoking cessation; **vaccination** (pneumococcal conjugate/polysaccharide, annual influenza, COVID-19, RSV for older adults).

#### Specific syndromes

- **Pneumocystis pneumonia (PJP):** HIV (CD4 <200), transplant, haematological malignancy, prolonged steroids; **dry cough, progressive dyspnoea, exertional desaturation,** bilateral ground-glass infiltrates, raised LDH, **β-D-glucan,** diagnosis by PCR/immunofluorescence of BAL or induced sputum; treat with **high-dose co-trimoxazole (21 days) plus prednisolone** if PaO₂ <9.3 kPa (70 mmHg); prophylaxis in at-risk patients.
- **Legionnaires disease:** consider with hyponatraemia, diarrhoea, confusion, hepatic enzymes; urinary antigen; fluoroquinolone or azithromycin; notify public health.
- **Fungal pneumonias** (histoplasmosis, coccidioidomycosis, aspergillosis: invasive in neutropenia (halo sign), chronic pulmonary, and allergic bronchopulmonary forms), **parasitic** (strongyloides hyperinfection in steroid-treated), **melioidosis** (South-East Asia/Australia).
- **COVID-19 pneumonia** and other viral pneumonias: oxygen, dexamethasone, antivirals (nirmatrelvir–ritonavir, remdesivir) and immunomodulators per guideline; anticoagulation; vaccination.

### 46.3 Asthma

#### Definition and diagnosis

**Asthma** is a heterogeneous chronic inflammatory airway disease characterised by **variable respiratory symptoms (wheeze, shortness of breath, chest tightness, cough)** that vary in time and intensity, **together with variable expiratory airflow limitation.** Diagnosis is **clinical history plus objective demonstration of variability:**

- **Spirometry:** FEV₁/FVC <0.70 (or below the lower limit of normal) **with bronchodilator reversibility** (increase in FEV₁ ≥12% and ≥200 mL after salbutamol; >15% and 400 mL is typical of asthma), **or** excessive **peak flow variability** (>10% diurnal variation over 2 weeks), **or** significant improvement after 4 weeks of anti-inflammatory treatment, **or** **positive bronchial challenge** (methacholine/mannitol/exercise), **or FeNO ≥40–50 ppb** (supportive; indicates type 2 inflammation) and **blood eosinophils.** **Normal spirometry between episodes does not exclude asthma.**
- **Features supporting asthma:** symptoms worse at night/early morning, with exercise, cold air, allergen exposure, viral infections, laughing; atopy (eczema, rhinitis), family history; wheeze on examination (may be absent between attacks).
- **Differential diagnosis:** COPD, **vocal cord dysfunction/inducible laryngeal obstruction,** heart failure, bronchiectasis, GORD with cough, upper airway cough syndrome, **ABPA,** eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA), anxiety/dysfunctional breathing, central airway obstruction (tumour), foreign body, PE.
- **Phenotypes:** allergic, non-allergic, late-onset, exercise-induced, **aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease** (nasal polyps, aspirin sensitivity), **occupational asthma** (isocyanates, flour, latex, wood dust: symptoms improve away from work), **cough-variant asthma,** obesity-related, **type 2-high vs type 2-low inflammation.**

#### Management (GINA principles)

**Goals:** control symptoms, prevent exacerbations, maintain normal activity and lung function, minimise medication side effects.

- **Never treat adults and adolescents with SABA alone** (increases the risk of exacerbations and death): all patients need **ICS-containing treatment.**
- **GINA Track 1 (preferred):** **low-dose ICS–formoterol as the reliever** (anti-inflammatory reliever), with **maintenance and reliever therapy (MART)** at steps 3–4:

| Step | Treatment (Track 1) |
|---|---|
| **1–2** | **As-needed low-dose ICS–formoterol** |
| **3** | **MART with low-dose ICS–formoterol** (maintenance + reliever) |
| **4** | **MART with medium-dose ICS–formoterol** |
| **5** | Add **LAMA (tiotropium),** consider **biologic therapy** by phenotype (**anti-IgE (omalizumab), anti-IL-5 (mepolizumab, reslizumab), anti-IL-5Rα (benralizumab), anti-IL-4Rα (dupilumab), anti-TSLP (tezepelumab)**), **azithromycin** (off-label), low-dose oral corticosteroids (last resort); bronchial thermoplasty |

(Track 2: SABA reliever with regular low-dose ICS plus LABA; alternatives when MART is unsuitable.)

- **Assess control** (symptoms, night waking, reliever use >2 days/week, activity limitation; ACT/ACQ scores), **risk factors for exacerbation** (previous exacerbation, poor adherence, overuse of SABA, low FEV₁, smoking, obesity, comorbid conditions), **step up if uncontrolled (after checking diagnosis, inhaler technique, adherence, triggers, comorbidity),** step down after 3 months of good control.
- **Inhaler technique** and device choice (pMDI with spacer, DPI, soft-mist), **written asthma action plan,** trigger avoidance (allergens, smoking cessation, occupational exposures, avoid NSAIDs/beta-blockers if relevant), **allergen immunotherapy** in selected allergic asthma, treat rhinitis, GORD, obesity, anxiety, **vaccination** (influenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19), **yearly review.**
- **Exacerbations:** Chapter 40: oral prednisolone 40–50 mg for 5 days, short-acting bronchodilators, controlled oxygen, magnesium for severe; **step-up of controller therapy and review within 2–7 days.**
- **Special populations:** **pregnancy** (continue controllers; asthma may worsen/improve), **children** (separate guidance), **exercise-induced bronchoconstriction** (warm-up, ICS-formoterol before exercise), **elderly** (overlap with COPD: asthma–COPD overlap features), **occupational asthma** (early removal from exposure).

### 46.4 Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)

#### Definition and diagnosis

**COPD** is a common, preventable, treatable disease characterised by **persistent respiratory symptoms (dyspnoea, cough, sputum)** and **persistent airflow limitation** (**post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC <0.70**) due to airway and/or alveolar abnormalities, usually caused by **significant exposure to noxious particles or gases** (tobacco smoking is the main cause; also biomass fuel smoke, occupational dusts and fumes, air pollution, **alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency,** childhood respiratory disease, asthma).

- **Consider COPD** in anyone >35–40 with dyspnoea, chronic cough or sputum, recurrent lower respiratory infections, and exposure. **Spirometry is required for diagnosis.**
- **Emphysema** (alveolar destruction), **chronic bronchitis** (cough and sputum ≥3 months for 2 years), **small airway disease.**
- **Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency:** early-onset (<45 years) emphysema, basal predominance, family history, liver disease, non-smokers with emphysema: **measure AAT level;** augmentation therapy in selected patients.

#### Assessment

- **Airflow limitation severity (GOLD grades, post-bronchodilator FEV₁ % predicted):** **1 (mild) ≥80; 2 (moderate) 50–79; 3 (severe) 30–49; 4 (very severe) <30.**
- **Symptoms:** mMRC dyspnoea scale (Chapter 23), **CAT (COPD Assessment Test).**
- **Exacerbation history:** **GOLD groups:** **A** (0–1 moderate exacerbations not leading to hospitalisation; mMRC 0–1, CAT <10), **B** (same exacerbations, more symptoms), **E** (≥2 moderate exacerbations or ≥1 leading to hospitalisation).
- **Investigations:** spirometry with reversibility, **chest X-ray** (hyperinflation, bullae, exclude cancer), **CT** (emphysema, bronchiectasis, lung cancer screening), **FBC** (**eosinophils** guide ICS; polycythaemia; anaemia), **BMI,** **ECG/echo** (cor pulmonale, heart failure), **oximetry/ABG,** **AAT level,** **gas transfer.**
- **Comorbidities** are frequent and drive prognosis: cardiovascular disease, heart failure, lung cancer, osteoporosis, depression and anxiety, metabolic syndrome, GORD, sleep apnoea, cachexia/sarcopenia, bronchiectasis.

#### Management

- **Smoking cessation** is the **only intervention that slows decline:** brief advice, behavioural support, **nicotine replacement (combination), varenicline, bupropion;** e-cigarettes in some programmes.
- **Vaccination:** annual influenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19, RSV, pertussis/Tdap.
- **Pulmonary rehabilitation** (exercise training, education, self-management; improves dyspnoea, exercise capacity, and quality of life; reduces hospital readmission).
- **Inhaled pharmacotherapy (GOLD):**
  - **Group A:** a bronchodilator (long-acting preferred).
  - **Group B:** **LABA + LAMA** (dual bronchodilation).
  - **Group E:** **LABA + LAMA;** **add ICS if blood eosinophils ≥300 cells/µL** (or ≥100 with exacerbations despite LABA+LAMA, and especially asthma overlap) (triple therapy LABA+LAMA+ICS reduces exacerbations and mortality).
  - **Avoid ICS monotherapy;** pneumonia risk with ICS (counsel); **short-acting bronchodilators for rescue.**
  - **Check inhaler technique** and choose a device the patient can use.
- **Further therapy for persistent exacerbations:** **roflumilast** (FEV₁ <50% with chronic bronchitis), **azithromycin** 250 mg daily or 500 mg three times weekly (ex-smokers; check ECG QTc and hearing, risk of resistance), mucolytics, **dupilumab** (type 2 inflammation with eosinophils ≥300), **ensifentrine.**
- **Oxygen:** **long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT)** for **PaO₂ ≤7.3 kPa (55 mmHg)** (or SaO₂ ≤88%), or **7.3–8.0 kPa (55–60 mmHg) with cor pulmonale, polycythaemia (Hct >55%), or peripheral oedema,** measured twice at least 3 weeks apart when clinically stable and on optimal therapy, **used ≥15 hours/day;** **must not smoke** (fire risk); ambulatory oxygen for exertional desaturation in selected patients.
- **Home non-invasive ventilation (NIV)** for chronic hypercapnia (PaCO₂ >7 kPa persistent after an exacerbation) and **CPAP/NIV for overlap syndrome** (COPD + sleep apnoea).
- **Surgical and bronchoscopic options:** **lung volume reduction surgery** or **endobronchial valves/coils** in selected patients with severe emphysema; **bullectomy;** **lung transplantation.**
- **Exacerbations:** Chapter 40: increased dyspnoea, sputum volume or purulence; **oral prednisolone 30–40 mg for 5 days,** antibiotics if purulent sputum (e.g. amoxicillin, doxycycline), bronchodilators, controlled oxygen, NIV for acidosis; **self-management plans** with "rescue packs" in selected patients; early review after discharge (**pulmonary rehabilitation within 4 weeks**).
- **Palliative care and advance care planning:** breathlessness management (opioids, fans, pacing), anxiety and depression treatment, treatment escalation plans.
- **Lung cancer screening** for eligible smokers (low-dose CT).
- **Prognosis:** BODE index (BMI, airflow obstruction, dyspnoea, exercise capacity).

### 46.5 Tuberculosis (TB)

#### Epidemiology and pathogenesis

TB (*Mycobacterium tuberculosis*) remains one of the leading infectious killers worldwide (about 10 million new cases and 1.3 million deaths per year; highest burden in South-East Asia, Africa, and the Western Pacific). Transmission is **airborne** via droplet nuclei from patients with pulmonary (especially smear-positive) disease. Infection may be **cleared, contained as latent TB infection (LTBI)** (lifetime risk of reactivation about 5–10%, higher with HIV, immunosuppression, diabetes, silicosis, renal failure, malnutrition, smoking, young children), or progress to **active disease** (primary progressive or post-primary reactivation).

#### Clinical features

- **Pulmonary TB:** **cough >2–3 weeks,** sputum, **haemoptysis,** fever, **night sweats,** weight loss, malaise, chest pain, dyspnoea; signs often minimal. **Chest X-ray:** upper-lobe infiltrates, **cavitation,** fibrosis, hilar lymphadenopathy (primary), pleural effusion, **miliary pattern;** CT is more sensitive. **HIV co-infection:** atypical radiographs (lower-lobe, no cavitation, lymphadenopathy), more extrapulmonary disease, smear-negative.
- **Extrapulmonary TB (about 15–20%; more with HIV):** **lymph node (scrofula;** cervical nodes, "cold abscess"), **pleural** (lymphocytic exudate, high ADA), **miliary/disseminated,** **meningitis** (subacute headache, cranial nerve palsies, hydrocephalus; high mortality), **spinal (Pott disease:** back pain, kyphosis, paraspinal abscess, cord compression), **genitourinary** (sterile pyuria, haematuria), **abdominal/peritoneal,** **pericardial** (effusion, constriction), **osteoarticular, cutaneous, adrenal.**
- **Presentations in special groups:** children (primary complex; less infectious), older adults (atypical), immunosuppressed (disseminated), **IRIS** after starting ART.

#### Diagnosis

- **Microbiological confirmation is essential:**
  - **Sputum (at least two samples, one early morning):** **acid-fast bacilli smear,** **culture** (gold standard; liquid media in 1–6 weeks), and **rapid nucleic-acid amplification tests (NAAT): Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra** (detects *M. tuberculosis* and **rifampicin resistance** within 2 hours), line-probe assays, whole-genome sequencing; **drug susceptibility testing** on all positive cultures.
  - **Induced sputum, bronchoalveolar lavage, gastric aspirates** (children), **pleural fluid/biopsy** (ADA >40 U/L suggests TB; biopsy yields caseating granulomas), **lymph node FNA/biopsy,** **CSF** (lymphocytes, high protein, low glucose; Xpert on large volume), **urine, tissue.**
  - **Urine lipoarabinomannan (LAM)** in advanced HIV.
- **Latent TB:** **interferon-γ release assay (IGRA)** or **tuberculin skin test (TST)**; cannot distinguish latent from active disease; active TB must be excluded (symptoms, chest X-ray) before treating LTBI.
- **Always test for HIV** (and screen for diabetes, hepatitis B/C before treatment).

#### Treatment of drug-susceptible TB

- **Standard 6-month regimen:** **2 months of isoniazid (H), rifampicin (R), pyrazinamide (Z), ethambutol (E)** (intensive phase) followed by **4 months of isoniazid and rifampicin** (continuation phase); fixed-dose combinations; **pyridoxine (vitamin B6)** with isoniazid to prevent neuropathy; **directly observed therapy (DOT)/video-observed therapy** and patient support for adherence.
- **Extended regimens:** **TB meningitis, CNS TB, bone/joint TB** (9–12 months; steroids for meningitis and pericarditis); **4-month rifapentine–moxifloxacin regimen** is an alternative for selected patients.
- **Adverse effects and monitoring:** **hepatotoxicity** (isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide: baseline LFTs; stop if ALT >3 × ULN with symptoms or >5 × ULN; alcohol, pre-existing liver disease increase risk), **optic neuritis** (ethambutol: baseline and monthly visual acuity/colour vision), **peripheral neuropathy** (isoniazid), **rash,** **hyperuricaemia/gout** (pyrazinamide), **gastrointestinal upset,** **orange discolouration of secretions** (rifampicin), **drug interactions** (**rifampicin is a potent enzyme inducer:** reduces effectiveness of hormonal contraception, warfarin, DOACs, antiretrovirals (protease inhibitors, some NNRTIs), antiepileptics, ciclosporin, steroids), **adjust ART and anticoagulants.**
- **HIV co-infection:** start ART within **2 weeks of TB therapy if CD4 <50** (within 8 weeks for others); watch for **IRIS.**
- **Drug-resistant TB:**
  - **Rifampicin-resistant/MDR-TB (isoniazid + rifampicin resistance):** **6-month all-oral regimens (BPaLM: bedaquiline, pretomanid, linezolid, moxifloxacin)** or individualised 9–20 months regimens; specialist centres; monitoring (QTc, linezolid toxicity: myelosuppression, neuropathy).
  - **XDR-TB:** additional fluoroquinolone and bedaquiline/linezolid resistance.
- **Infection control:** airborne isolation (negative-pressure room) of infectious patients until 2 weeks of effective treatment and clinical improvement (longer for drug-resistant disease); N95/FFP3 masks for staff.
- **Public health:** **notification;** **contact tracing** (household and close contacts: symptom screen, chest X-ray, IGRA/TST; children <5 and immunosuppressed contacts treated for LTBI/window prophylaxis); **BCG vaccination** (protects children against severe forms).
- **Treatment of latent TB infection:** **rifapentine + isoniazid weekly for 12 weeks (3HP),** **rifampicin daily for 4 months (4R),** **isoniazid daily for 6–9 months (6H/9H);** priorities: contacts, people with HIV, those starting TNF inhibitors or other immunosuppression, transplant candidates, silicosis, recent converters.
- **Outcomes and follow-up:** sputum culture conversion at 2 months; monitor adherence and adverse effects; complete the full course; check for relapse; manage sequelae (post-TB bronchiectasis, aspergilloma, chronic obstructive disease).

### 46.6 Pleural diseases

#### Pleural effusion

- **Presentation:** dyspnoea, pleuritic pain (or none), dry cough; **stony dull percussion, reduced breath sounds.** **Confirm** with chest X-ray (blunted costophrenic angle ≥200 mL on PA film) and **thoracic ultrasound** (more sensitive; guides sampling).
- **Transudate vs exudate:** **Light's criteria:** **exudate** if **any** of: **pleural fluid protein/serum protein >0.5; pleural LDH/serum LDH >0.6; pleural LDH >two-thirds of the upper limit of normal serum LDH.** (Transudates may be misclassified in diuresed heart failure patients: use albumin gradient >12 g/L or NT-proBNP.)

| | **Transudate** | **Exudate** |
|---|---|---|
| **Causes** | **Heart failure** (most common; bilateral, right > left), **cirrhosis** (hepatic hydrothorax), nephrotic syndrome, hypoalbuminaemia, peritoneal dialysis, hypothyroidism, SVC obstruction, constrictive pericarditis, atelectasis | **Parapneumonic/empyema, malignancy** (lung, breast, lymphoma, mesothelioma, metastases), **TB, pulmonary embolism,** pancreatitis, connective tissue disease (RA, SLE), post-MI/CABG (Dressler), asbestos-related benign effusion, drugs (amiodarone, nitrofurantoin, methotrexate), chylothorax, haemothorax, uraemia, yellow nail syndrome, sarcoidosis, Meigs syndrome (ovarian fibroma) |

- **Pleural fluid tests:** protein, LDH, glucose, **pH** (<7.2: complicated parapneumonic/empyema, also in rheumatoid, malignancy, TB, oesophageal rupture), cell count and differential, **cytology** (sensitivity about 60%), **Gram stain and culture,** AFB, **ADA** (TB), amylase (pancreatitis, oesophageal rupture), triglycerides/cholesterol (chylothorax/pseudochylothorax), NT-proBNP, haematocrit (haemothorax: pleural Hct >50% of blood).
- **Management:** treat the cause; **therapeutic thoracentesis** for symptomatic relief (limit to 1–1.5 L at a time to avoid re-expansion pulmonary oedema), **chest drain** for empyema/complicated effusion; **malignant pleural effusion:** **indwelling pleural catheter** (outpatient drainage) or **talc pleurodesis;** **thoracoscopy (medical or VATS)** for undiagnosed exudates (biopsy), TB (anti-TB therapy), **empyema** (antibiotics, drainage, intrapleural fibrinolytics, surgery).
- **Hepatic hydrothorax:** salt restriction, diuretics, TIPS; avoid chest drains.

#### Pneumothorax

- **Primary spontaneous pneumothorax (PSP):** young, tall, thin men; smoking; blebs; sudden pleuritic pain and dyspnoea. **Secondary (SSP):** underlying lung disease (COPD, cystic fibrosis, TB, ILD, lung cancer, Marfan, catamenial, PJP); more dangerous.
- **Diagnosis:** chest X-ray (visible visceral pleural edge; size measured at hilum: **small <2 cm, large ≥2 cm**), ultrasound, CT; **tension pneumothorax** (clinical diagnosis: Chapter 37).
- **Management (BTS 2023):**
  - **PSP:** **small, minimally symptomatic: observation (outpatient review in 2–4 days);** **large or symptomatic: needle aspiration (success ~50–60%) or small-bore chest drain (10–14 Fr);** large-bore drains are rarely needed.
  - **SSP:** admit; **chest drain** (or aspiration if <2 cm, minimal symptoms); oxygen; early respiratory review; treat underlying disease.
  - **Persistent air leak (>5 days) or recurrence:** **surgical referral (VATS bullectomy and pleurodesis/pleurectomy);** surgery after the first episode for high-risk occupations (pilots, divers) and bilateral/haemothorax.
  - **Advice:** stop smoking; **avoid flying until radiological resolution (traditionally 2–6 weeks, per local guidance); no diving unless definitively treated.**
- **Mesothelioma:** asbestos exposure (latency 20–50 years), chest pain, dyspnoea, unilateral effusion/pleural thickening; CT, thoracoscopic biopsy; chemo-immunotherapy; compensation.
- **Pleural plaques:** benign marker of asbestos exposure.

### 46.7 Interstitial lung disease (ILD)

- **Features:** progressive exertional dyspnoea, dry cough, **bibasal fine "Velcro" crackles,** clubbing (IPF, asbestosis), restrictive spirometry (reduced FVC, normal/high ratio), **reduced DLCO,** exertional desaturation; **HRCT** is central (patterns: UIP, NSIP, organising pneumonia, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, sarcoid, LAM, PLCH).
- **Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF):** age >60, male predominance, smoking; **UIP pattern on HRCT** (basal, subpleural reticulation, honeycombing, traction bronchiectasis) (biopsy often unnecessary with typical HRCT and MDT consensus); **antifibrotics: nintedanib or pirfenidone** slow FVC decline; oxygen, pulmonary rehabilitation, GERD management, vaccination, palliative care, **lung transplant referral**; acute exacerbations carry high mortality; median survival 3–5 years.
- **Other ILDs:** **hypersensitivity pneumonitis** (bird fanciers, farmers, moulds: remove antigen, steroids), **connective tissue disease–ILD** (systemic sclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis, myositis: immunosuppression), **drug-induced** (amiodarone, methotrexate, nitrofurantoin, bleomycin, checkpoint inhibitors), **occupational** (asbestosis, silicosis, coal worker's pneumoconiosis), **organising pneumonia** (steroids), **eosinophilic pneumonia,** **sarcoidosis.**
- **Sarcoidosis:** multisystem non-caseating granulomatous disease; **bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy** (stage I), parenchymal disease (II–IV), **Löfgren syndrome** (erythema nodosum, bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy, arthritis/ankle periarthritis, fever: good prognosis), uveitis, skin lesions (lupus pernio), hypercalcaemia, cardiac and neurological involvement, raised ACE (non-specific); biopsy (EBUS-TBNA); **corticosteroids** for symptomatic or organ-threatening disease; second-line immunosuppressants.
- **Multidisciplinary discussion (respiratory, radiology, pathology, rheumatology)** is essential for diagnosis.

### 46.8 Lung cancer and pulmonary nodules

- **Risk factors:** **smoking** (about 85%), passive smoke, radon, asbestos, occupational carcinogens, air pollution, family history, COPD/fibrosis.
- **Types:** **non-small cell** (adenocarcinoma, squamous, large cell) about 85%; **small cell** (central, rapid, early metastases, paraneoplastic syndromes).
- **Presentation:** cough, haemoptysis, dyspnoea, chest pain, weight loss, hoarseness, recurrent pneumonia, clubbing, **Pancoast syndrome** (shoulder pain, Horner, hand wasting), **SVC obstruction,** pleural effusion, bone/brain/liver/adrenal metastases, **paraneoplastic** (SIADH, hypercalcaemia, Cushing, Lambert–Eaton, hypertrophic osteoarthropathy).
- **Diagnosis and staging:** **chest X-ray** (urgent suspected-cancer pathway for features like haemoptysis, persistent cough, clubbing, abnormal X-ray), **CT chest/upper abdomen,** **PET-CT,** **biopsy** (bronchoscopy/EBUS-TBNA for central/nodal disease; CT-guided percutaneous for peripheral), **mediastinal staging,** **brain MRI;** **molecular testing** (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET, KRAS G12C, RET, NTRK; PD-L1) guides targeted therapy and immunotherapy.
- **Treatment:** **surgery (lobectomy) or stereotactic body radiotherapy** for early-stage NSCLC (fitness assessment: FEV₁, DLCO); **chemoradiotherapy ± consolidation immunotherapy (durvalumab)** for locally advanced; **targeted therapy** (osimertinib for EGFR; alectinib for ALK), **immunotherapy ± chemotherapy** (pembrolizumab etc.) for advanced disease; **small cell:** limited-stage chemoradiotherapy, extensive-stage chemo-immunotherapy; palliative care early.
- **Screening:** **annual low-dose CT** for high-risk individuals (e.g. age 50–80, ≥20 pack-years, current smoker or quit within 15 years) reduces lung cancer mortality (NLST, NELSON); smoking cessation support.
- **Pulmonary nodule (solitary, <3 cm):** assess **size, density (solid, part-solid, ground-glass), margins (spiculated), location (upper lobe), growth,** patient risk (age, smoking, prior cancer); **Fleischner/BTS guidance:** low-risk small nodules: follow-up CT at intervals; **PET-CT/biopsy/resection** for nodules ≥8–10 mm or growing; stability for 2 years in solid nodules (not for ground-glass nodules: longer follow-up).

### 46.9 Bronchiectasis, cystic fibrosis, and sleep apnoea

#### Bronchiectasis

- **Permanent dilatation of the bronchi** with chronic inflammation and infection; **chronic productive cough (daily purulent sputum), recurrent infections, haemoptysis, dyspnoea, wheeze, clubbing, coarse crackles.** **HRCT** (signet-ring sign, bronchial wall thickening, tram tracks).
- **Causes:** **post-infectious** (TB, severe childhood pneumonia, pertussis), **cystic fibrosis,** **primary ciliary dyskinesia,** **immunodeficiency** (hypogammaglobulinaemia, HIV), **ABPA,** **rheumatoid arthritis,** **COPD/asthma,** **inflammatory bowel disease,** α1-antitrypsin deficiency, aspiration, obstruction, **idiopathic.**
- **Work-up:** sputum cultures (*Pseudomonas aeruginosa,* *H. influenzae,* NTM), **immunoglobulins and vaccine responses,** *Aspergillus* IgE/precipitins, **sweat chloride/CFTR genetics,** ciliary assessment, spirometry, rheumatoid factor, HIV.
- **Treatment:** **airway clearance** (physiotherapy techniques, mucolytics, hypertonic saline), **exacerbations:** antibiotics for 14 days guided by cultures (higher doses, longer duration for *Pseudomonas*), **long-term macrolide** (azithromycin) or inhaled antibiotics (colistin, gentamicin) for ≥3 exacerbations/year or colonisation, **pulmonary rehabilitation, vaccination,** treat the cause, bronchial artery embolisation for haemoptysis; surgery for localised disease.

#### Cystic fibrosis (CF)

- Autosomal recessive **CFTR** mutations (F508del most common): **recurrent chest infections, bronchiectasis, pancreatic insufficiency (steatorrhoea, failure to thrive), meconium ileus (neonates), diabetes, liver disease, male infertility, nasal polyps, salt loss.** **Newborn screening, sweat chloride >60 mmol/L.** **CFTR modulators** (elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor) have transformed outcomes; airway clearance, nutrition, enzyme replacement, infection management; transplantation.

#### Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA)

- **Recurrent upper airway collapse in sleep** with snoring, witnessed apnoeas, nocturnal gasping, daytime sleepiness, morning headaches, concentration problems, nocturia, hypertension, AF, increased cardiovascular and accident risk; **risk factors:** obesity, male sex, older age, neck circumference, craniofacial anatomy, menopause, alcohol/sedatives, hypothyroidism, acromegaly.
- **Screening:** **STOP-BANG,** Epworth Sleepiness Scale; **diagnosis:** home sleep apnoea testing or polysomnography; **apnoea–hypopnoea index (AHI):** **mild 5–14, moderate 15–29, severe ≥30 events/hour.**
- **Treatment:** **CPAP** (first-line for moderate–severe or symptomatic; adherence ≥4 hours/night), **weight loss, avoid alcohol/sedatives, positional therapy, mandibular advancement devices, hypoglossal nerve stimulation, bariatric and ENT surgery;** **driving advice** (stop driving if sleepy until controlled).
- **Obesity hypoventilation** and **overlap syndrome** (Chapter 40).

### 46.10 Other conditions in brief

- **Aspergillus lung disease:** **ABPA** (asthma/CF patients: wheeze, brown mucus plugs, high IgE and eosinophils, central bronchiectasis; steroids ± itraconazole), **aspergilloma** (cavity with fungus ball: haemoptysis; embolisation, surgery), **invasive aspergillosis** (neutropenia: halo sign; voriconazole/isavuconazole), **chronic pulmonary aspergillosis.**
- **Non-tuberculous mycobacterial (NTM) disease:** *M. avium* complex (bronchiectasis, older women, "Lady Windermere"): prolonged multi-drug therapy.
- **Occupational lung disease:** asbestosis/mesothelioma, silicosis (TB risk), coal worker's pneumoconiosis, byssinosis, occupational asthma, hypersensitivity pneumonitis; **take an occupational history in every respiratory patient** (Chapter 7); notify and refer for compensation.
- **Pulmonary embolism and pulmonary hypertension:** Chapter 45.
- **Acute respiratory failure and ARDS:** Chapter 40.
- **Lung transplantation referral:** advanced disease with a limited prognosis, no major contraindications.
- **Cough and haemoptysis:** Chapter 23.

### 46.11 Worked cases

#### Case 1: Community-acquired pneumonia: severity and treatment

*A 74-year-old man has 3 days of fever, productive cough and confusion. RR 32, BP 96/54, SpO₂ 88% on air, urea 11.2 mmol/L. Chest X-ray: right lower lobe consolidation. Urinary pneumococcal antigen positive.*

**Reasoning.** **CURB-65 = 5** (confusion, urea >7, RR ≥30, BP <90/60 diastolic, age ≥65): **severe pneumococcal CAP** with respiratory failure.

**Action.** Oxygen, IV fluids, blood cultures, **IV co-amoxiclav or ceftriaxone plus clarithromycin** (cover atypicals), consider **hydrocortisone** for severe CAP, ICU/HDU assessment (CPAP/HFNO), VTE prophylaxis; rationalise to penicillin/amoxicillin after antigen/culture; total 7 days; **follow-up chest X-ray in 6 weeks.**

#### Case 2: Failure to respond: empyema

*A 52-year-old woman with CAP improves slightly but continues to spike fevers on day 6 with pleuritic pain. Ultrasound: large complex loculated effusion. Aspirate: pus, pH 6.9, glucose 1.0 mmol/L.*

**Reasoning.** **Parapneumonic empyema.**

**Action.** **Small-bore chest drain,** continue antibiotics (broad-spectrum with anaerobic cover), **intrapleural tPA and DNase** if inadequate drainage, surgical referral (VATS) if unresolved after 5–7 days, nutrition.

#### Case 3: Undiagnosed adult-onset asthma

*A 36-year-old woman has 6 months of night cough, wheeze after exercise and exposure to cats, with eczema and hay fever. Spirometry: FEV₁/FVC 0.68, FEV₁ increases by 18% (480 mL) after salbutamol. FeNO 62 ppb; eosinophils 0.5 × 10⁹/L.*

**Reasoning.** **Allergic asthma** with variable airflow limitation.

**Action.** **Low-dose ICS-formoterol as needed (or MART),** inhaler technique, written action plan, allergen avoidance, treat rhinitis, review in 4–8 weeks; step up if poorly controlled; consider biologics only for severe asthma.

#### Case 4: COPD: triple therapy decision

*A 66-year-old ex-smoker (45 pack-years) has chronic dyspnoea (mMRC 3), CAT 24, and two moderate exacerbations in the last year. Post-bronchodilator FEV₁ 42% predicted, FEV₁/FVC 0.48. Blood eosinophils 320 cells/µL.*

**Reasoning.** **COPD GOLD 3, group E, eosinophils ≥300.**

**Action.** **LABA + LAMA + ICS (triple therapy)** (consider single-inhaler), pulmonary rehabilitation, vaccinations, smoking cessation support (if relapse), rescue pack, azithromycin or roflumilast if exacerbations persist; screen for hypoxia, comorbidities, depression, osteoporosis (ICS).

#### Case 5: Tuberculosis

*A 31-year-old man from a high-incidence country has 6 weeks of cough, night sweats, 7 kg weight loss and haemoptysis. Chest X-ray: right upper-lobe cavity. Sputum smear AFB positive; Xpert MTB/RIF: *M. tuberculosis* detected, rifampicin resistance not detected. HIV negative.*

**Reasoning.** **Smear-positive pulmonary TB, rifampicin-susceptible.**

**Action.** Airborne isolation; **2HRZE/4HR** with pyridoxine; baseline LFTs and visual acuity; DOT; notification and **contact tracing;** culture and full susceptibility; HIV and diabetes screening; follow-up sputum at 2 months.

#### Case 6: Pleural effusion: malignant

*A 68-year-old woman, a smoker, has progressive breathlessness and weight loss. Chest X-ray: large right pleural effusion. Ultrasound-guided aspiration: straw-coloured exudate (protein 42 g/L, LDH high), pH 7.31; cytology: adenocarcinoma cells.*

**Reasoning.** **Malignant pleural effusion** from lung adenocarcinoma.

**Action.** Therapeutic drainage, **indwelling pleural catheter** (or talc pleurodesis), CT staging, molecular testing, oncology MDT, palliative care.

#### Case 7: Spontaneous pneumothorax

*A 21-year-old tall thin man, smoker, has sudden left pleuritic pain and mild breathlessness. SpO₂ 98%, HR 88. Chest X-ray: 3-cm apical pneumothorax.*

**Reasoning.** **Primary spontaneous pneumothorax, large (≥2 cm).**

**Action.** Needle aspiration (or small-bore drain); repeat X-ray; discharge when re-expanded with advice (smoking cessation, avoid flying/diving); surgical referral if recurrence.

### 46.12 Common pitfalls

- **Treating all cough with fever as bacterial pneumonia;** missing PE, heart failure, lung cancer, TB, ILD, aspiration.
- **Not calculating CURB-65 and ignoring social factors;** under- or over-estimating severity.
- **Forgetting atypical and *Legionella* cover in severe CAP;** not using urinary antigens.
- **Prolonged antibiotics beyond 5–7 days** without need; **failure to arrange follow-up imaging** in smokers.
- **Missing empyema** in a patient who fails to respond.
- **Diagnosing asthma without objective testing;** prescribing SABA alone; ignoring inhaler technique and adherence.
- **Diagnosing COPD without spirometry;** ignoring smoking cessation; ICS monotherapy; failing to refer for rehabilitation.
- **Missing alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency** in young emphysema.
- **Delaying TB diagnosis** (not thinking of TB; relying on smear alone; not requesting NAAT); starting treatment without HIV testing; ignoring drug interactions with rifampicin.
- **Draining large effusions rapidly** (re-expansion oedema); missing pleural infection; misclassifying transudates in diuresed patients.
- **Large-bore drains for simple pneumothorax;** failing to advise on flying and diving.
- **Missing lung cancer on the chest X-ray;** not following up nodules.
- **Missing sleep apnoea** in resistant hypertension and AF.

### 46.13 Key points

- Pneumonia: assess with CURB-65, treat early with guideline antibiotics (amoxicillin for low severity; add macrolide/doxycycline for moderate; beta-lactam plus macrolide for severe), reassess at 72 hours, think of complications (empyema) and alternative diagnoses, and follow up smokers.
- Asthma: variable symptoms plus variable airflow limitation; anti-inflammatory therapy for all (ICS-formoterol as reliever/MART); biologics for severe type 2 asthma; action plans.
- COPD: spirometry-confirmed (post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC <0.7); smoking cessation, rehabilitation, LABA+LAMA ± ICS by eosinophils and exacerbations; LTOT and NIV by criteria.
- TB: think of it; confirm microbiologically with NAAT and culture; 2HRZE/4HR; test HIV; notify and trace contacts; treat latent TB in at-risk groups.
- Pleural effusion: Light's criteria and pleural fluid analysis; pneumothorax: size and symptoms guide aspiration versus observation versus drain.
- ILD needs HRCT and MDT; IPF treated with antifibrotics; sarcoidosis often resolves; lung cancer: urgent imaging and molecular profiling; screening with low-dose CT in high-risk smokers.
- Bronchiectasis requires airway clearance and treatment of causes; OSA needs diagnosis and CPAP.

### 46.14 Self-assessment

1. Calculate CURB-65 for a 68-year-old with new confusion, RR 28, BP 100/60, urea 9 mmol/L. What does the score imply?
   *Answer: Confusion 1 + urea >7 1 + age ≥65 1 = 3 (RR <30, BP not <90/≤60): severe pneumonia with high mortality; admit, consider HDU/ICU, IV antibiotics.*

2. State the GINA diagnostic criteria for asthma and the preferred reliever.
   *Answer: Variable respiratory symptoms plus variable expiratory airflow limitation (reversibility, PEF variability, positive challenge, or response to treatment); low-dose ICS-formoterol is the preferred anti-inflammatory reliever (never SABA alone).*

3. When is long-term oxygen indicated in COPD?
   *Answer: Stable PaO₂ ≤7.3 kPa (55 mmHg), or 7.3–8.0 kPa with cor pulmonale, polycythaemia or peripheral oedema, used ≥15 hours/day, with no smoking.*

4. Outline the standard regimen for drug-susceptible pulmonary TB and key monitoring.
   *Answer: 2 months of isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide and ethambutol, then 4 months of isoniazid and rifampicin, with pyridoxine; monitor LFTs, visual acuity, adherence, and drug interactions; test HIV; notify and trace contacts.*

5. Apply Light's criteria: pleural protein 38 g/L, serum protein 70 g/L; pleural LDH 400 U/L, serum LDH 200 U/L (ULN 250).
   *Answer: Protein ratio 0.54 (>0.5) and LDH ratio 2.0 (>0.6): exudate.*

6. What are the management options for a primary spontaneous pneumothorax of 3 cm?
   *Answer: Needle aspiration or small-bore chest drain (observation only if small and minimally symptomatic), with follow-up, advice about smoking, flying and diving, and surgery for recurrence.*
