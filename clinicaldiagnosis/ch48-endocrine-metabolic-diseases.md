## Chapter 48. Endocrine and Metabolic Diseases

### Learning objectives

After studying this chapter you should be able to:

1. Diagnose and classify diabetes mellitus (type 1, type 2, gestational, monogenic and secondary forms), and apply glycaemic targets, drug selection and complication screening.
2. Evaluate thyroid function tests, and diagnose and manage hyperthyroidism, hypothyroidism, thyroiditis, goitre and nodules.
3. Recognise and investigate adrenal disorders (Cushing syndrome, primary aldosteronism, adrenal insufficiency, phaeochromocytoma, incidentaloma).
4. Evaluate pituitary disease (hypopituitarism, prolactinoma, acromegaly) and calcium and parathyroid disorders.
5. Diagnose and manage obesity, dyslipidaemia, osteoporosis and gout in primary care and specialist settings.
6. Interpret screening and confirmatory tests, using pre-test probability, timing and interfering factors.
7. Recognise endocrine emergencies and the principles of long-term follow-up and complication prevention.

---

### 48.1 Approach to endocrine disease

Endocrine disorders are **frequent, treatable and often missed** because their symptoms (fatigue, weight change, mood change, menstrual disturbance, sweating, palpitations, polyuria) are non-specific. The logic of diagnosis:

1. **Suspect from the clinical picture** (history, examination, associated conditions).
2. **Measure the hormone and its regulator** together: a **high hormone with a suppressed regulator** (e.g. low TSH and high T4) indicates a primary gland disorder; **both low** suggests pituitary disease; an **inappropriately normal regulator** is also informative.
3. **Use dynamic testing** when baseline results are equivocal: **suppression tests** for hormone excess (dexamethasone for cortisol, oral glucose for growth hormone, saline for aldosterone) and **stimulation tests** for deficiency (ACTH/synacthen, insulin tolerance, glucagon).
4. **Localise** (imaging, venous sampling) only **after** biochemical confirmation.
5. **Consider interfering factors:** drugs (steroids, oestrogen, biotin, amiodarone, heparin, lithium), non-thyroidal illness, pregnancy, timing/circadian rhythm, stress, assay problems.
6. **Treat the cause, replace deficiency, long-term monitoring.**

### 48.2 Diabetes mellitus

#### Diagnosis (WHO/ADA)

| Test | Diabetes | Prediabetes |
|---|---|---|
| **Fasting plasma glucose** | ≥7.0 mmol/L (126 mg/dL) | 6.1–6.9 (110–125; ADA 5.6–6.9) |
| **2-hour glucose, 75 g OGTT** | ≥11.1 mmol/L (200 mg/dL) | 7.8–11.0 |
| **HbA1c** | **≥48 mmol/mol (6.5%)** | 42–47 (6.0–6.4; ADA 5.7–6.4) |
| **Random glucose with classic symptoms** | ≥11.1 mmol/L (200 mg/dL) | |

In asymptomatic patients, **confirm with a repeat test** (same or different test); in symptomatic patients or DKA, one abnormal test suffices. **HbA1c is unreliable** in haemoglobinopathies, anaemia, haemolysis, recent transfusion, pregnancy, CKD, and rapidly developing diabetes (type 1): use glucose criteria.

#### Classification

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **Type 1 diabetes (T1D)** | Autoimmune β-cell destruction; **onset in childhood or young adulthood** (any age); **polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, ketosis/DKA;** lean; **autoantibodies** (GAD65, IA-2, ZnT8, insulin), low C-peptide; lifelong insulin; associated autoimmune disease (thyroid, coeliac, Addison) |
| **Type 2 diabetes (T2D)** | Insulin resistance with relative insulin deficiency; **obesity, family history, older age, ethnicity,** metabolic syndrome; gradual onset, often asymptomatic at diagnosis; complications may be present at diagnosis; increasing in young people |
| **Gestational diabetes (GDM)** | First recognised in pregnancy (OGTT at 24–28 weeks; earlier if high risk); risk of macrosomia, shoulder dystocia, neonatal hypoglycaemia, pre-eclampsia; lifelong risk of T2D (screen at 6–13 weeks postpartum and annually) |
| **Monogenic (MODY)** | Autosomal dominant, young onset (<25–35), non-obese, strong family history, no autoantibodies, preserved C-peptide; **HNF1A (sulfonylurea-sensitive), GCK (mild, stable, no treatment)**; neonatal diabetes (<6 months: KCNJ11/ABCC8, sulfonylureas) |
| **Secondary** | Pancreatic disease (chronic pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis, haemochromatosis, pancreatic cancer: type 3c), **endocrine** (Cushing, acromegaly, phaeochromocytoma, glucagonoma), **drugs** (glucocorticoids, antipsychotics, tacrolimus, thiazides, statins, checkpoint inhibitors causing fulminant T1D), post-transplant |
| **Latent autoimmune diabetes in adults (LADA)** | Adult-onset, autoantibody-positive, slow progression to insulin |

**Prediabetes:** impaired fasting glucose and/or impaired glucose tolerance: lifestyle intervention (Diabetes Prevention Programme: 5–7% weight loss and activity reduces progression by ~58%), metformin in selected.

#### Management of type 2 diabetes

**Principles (ADA/EASD):** individualised, **weight- and cardiorenal-protection-centred,** with education and self-management.

- **Education and lifestyle:** structured education, **Mediterranean-type diet, reduce refined carbohydrates,** weight loss (≥5–15%; remission possible with ≥10–15% weight loss), **150 minutes/week activity,** smoking cessation, alcohol moderation, sleep.
- **Glycaemic targets:** **HbA1c ≈53 mmol/mol (7.0%)** for most; 48 (6.5%) if achievable without hypoglycaemia; **58–64 (7.5–8.0%)** in frail older adults or those with limited life expectancy/hypoglycaemia risk. Self-monitoring or **continuous glucose monitoring** (CGM) for insulin users.
- **Drug choice:**
  - **Metformin** first-line (check eGFR; stop if <30; B12 monitoring; GI effects; lactic acidosis rare).
  - **If established atherosclerotic CVD, heart failure, or CKD (regardless of HbA1c): add an SGLT2 inhibitor (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin) and/or a GLP-1 receptor agonist (semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide)** for cardiorenal protection; **GLP-1 RAs and tirzepatide (GIP/GLP-1)** for weight-centred management (obesity).
  - Others: **DPP-4 inhibitors** (weight-neutral, low hypoglycaemia; avoid saxagliptin in HF), **sulfonylureas** (hypoglycaemia, weight gain: cheap, effective), **pioglitazone** (avoid in HF, fractures, bladder cancer concern), **insulin** (basal first: 10 units or 0.1–0.2 units/kg; titrate; add prandial or GLP-1 RA), **acarbose.**
  - **Start insulin when:** symptomatic hyperglycaemia/catabolism, HbA1c >86 mmol/mol (10%), persistent failure of other therapy, pregnancy.
  - **SGLT2 inhibitor safety:** genital mycotic infections, volume depletion, **euglycaemic DKA** (stop during acute illness/fasting/surgery), Fournier gangrene (rare), amputation signal (canagliflozin).
- **Cardiovascular risk management:** **blood pressure target <130/80,** ACEi/ARB if albuminuria, **statin** (moderate–high intensity; LDL targets <1.8 mmol/L for high-risk, <1.4 very high risk), **antiplatelet** in established CVD (not routine primary prevention), smoking cessation.
- **Bariatric/metabolic surgery** for BMI ≥35 (≥30–32.5 with diabetes in many guidelines): high rates of remission.
- **Hypoglycaemia** (Chapter 43): avoid with sulfonylureas/insulin in older adults.

#### Management of type 1 diabetes

- **Insulin** lifelong: **basal-bolus** (long-acting basal + rapid-acting with meals; carbohydrate counting), **insulin pumps** and **automated insulin delivery (closed-loop)** systems, **CGM.** Targets: HbA1c ≈48–53 mmol/mol (6.5–7.0%), time in range (3.9–10 mmol/L) >70%, time below range <4%.
- **Sick-day rules,** ketone monitoring, DKA education, hypoglycaemia management (glucagon), driving safety, exercise, alcohol, pregnancy planning (HbA1c <48, folic acid 5 mg, stop ACEi/statins), annual screening for coeliac disease, thyroid disease, **psychological support (diabetes distress, eating disorders, insulin omission)**, **teplizumab** to delay onset in stage 2 T1D.

#### Complications and screening

| Complication | Features | Screening and management |
|---|---|---|
| **Retinopathy** (non-proliferative, proliferative, maculopathy) | Often asymptomatic until advanced; leading cause of blindness in working age | **Annual retinal screening** (T2D from diagnosis; T1D from 12 years or puberty at 5 years); laser, anti-VEGF injections, vitrectomy; tight BP and glucose control |
| **Nephropathy (diabetic kidney disease)** | Albuminuria, falling eGFR | **Annual eGFR and urine albumin:creatinine ratio (uACR);** ACEi/ARB, **SGLT2 inhibitor, finerenone (non-steroidal MRA),** GLP-1 RA, BP control |
| **Neuropathy** | Distal symmetrical sensory (burning, numbness), autonomic (postural hypotension, gastroparesis, erectile dysfunction, silent MI), mononeuropathies | **Annual foot examination (monofilament, vibration);** pain: duloxetine, pregabalin, amitriptyline, topical capsaicin/lidocaine |
| **Diabetic foot** | Neuropathic and ischaemic ulcers, Charcot arthropathy, infection, osteomyelitis | **Foot protection and education,** footwear, **multidisciplinary foot clinic;** urgent treatment of infection (osteomyelitis: MRI); offloading; revascularisation |
| **Macrovascular disease** | MI, stroke, peripheral arterial disease (most common cause of death) | Risk factor control (statin, BP, SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA, antiplatelet as indicated) |
| **Other** | Cheiroarthropathy, NAFLD/MASLD, periodontal disease, depression, cognitive impairment, skin infections, fungal infections, sleep apnoea | Screening and treatment |

#### Diabetes in specific situations

- **Hospital:** target glucose 7.8–10 mmol/L (140–180 mg/dL), basal-bolus insulin (avoid sliding scale alone), peri-operative management (stop SGLT2i 3–4 days before surgery), steroid-induced hyperglycaemia.
- **Pregnancy:** **pre-gestational** and **gestational** (diet, metformin, insulin; targets fasting <5.3, 1-hour <7.8, 2-hour <6.4 mmol/L), fetal surveillance.
- **Older adults:** simplify regimens, avoid hypoglycaemia, relax targets.
- **Mental health:** antipsychotic-related metabolic syndrome.

### 48.3 Thyroid disease

#### Thyroid function tests (TFTs)

| TSH | Free T4 | Free T3 | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low (suppressed)** | **High** | High | **Primary hyperthyroidism** (Graves, toxic nodule, thyroiditis) |
| **Low** | Normal | **High** | **T3 toxicosis** |
| **Low** | Normal | Normal | **Subclinical hyperthyroidism** (also non-thyroidal illness, drugs: steroids, dopamine, biotin interference) |
| **High** | **Low** | Low | **Primary hypothyroidism** |
| **High** | Normal | Normal | **Subclinical hypothyroidism** (repeat in 6–12 weeks) |
| **Low/normal** | **Low** | Low | **Central (secondary) hypothyroidism** or non-thyroidal illness: check other pituitary hormones |
| **High** | High | High | **TSH-secreting adenoma, thyroid hormone resistance, assay interference** |

**Interfering factors:** biotin (false results), heterophile antibodies, heparin (falsely high free T4), recovery from illness (transient TSH rise), pregnancy (reference ranges lower: trimester-specific TSH), amiodarone, lithium, iodine contrast, drugs (steroids, dopamine suppress TSH).

#### Hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis)

- **Causes:** **Graves' disease** (TSH-receptor antibodies; young women; diffuse goitre, **ophthalmopathy, pretibial myxoedema, thyroid acropachy**), **toxic multinodular goitre and toxic adenoma** (older, iodine-deficient regions), **thyroiditis** (subacute (de Quervain: painful, post-viral), postpartum, silent/painless, drug-induced: amiodarone, immune checkpoint inhibitors, interferon), **iodine-induced (Jod-Basedow),** exogenous levothyroxine, struma ovarii, trophoblastic disease/hCG, TSH-secreting adenoma, factitious.
- **Symptoms and signs:** weight loss with increased appetite, **heat intolerance, sweating, tremor, palpitations, anxiety, irritability, diarrhoea, oligomenorrhoea, proximal myopathy, tachycardia/AF, warm moist skin, lid lag, onycholysis, hyperreflexia;** **older adults: apathetic thyrotoxicosis (weight loss, AF, heart failure, depression).**
- **Investigation:** TFTs, **TSH-receptor antibodies (TRAb)** (diagnostic of Graves), **thyroid scintigraphy/uptake** (high diffuse uptake: Graves; focal: toxic adenoma; **low uptake: thyroiditis or exogenous hormone**), thyroid ultrasound (nodules), ECG, bone health; pregnancy test.
- **Treatment:**
  - **Symptom control:** **beta-blocker** (propranolol, atenolol).
  - **Antithyroid drugs (ATDs):** **carbimazole/methimazole** (first-line; **propylthiouracil in first trimester of pregnancy and thyroid storm**), **block-and-replace or titration regimen for 12–18 months** (Graves): relapse about 50%; **agranulocytosis** (warn patients to stop and seek help for fever/sore throat: baseline FBC), hepatitis (PTU), vasculitis, rash, teratogenicity (carbimazole: first trimester).
  - **Definitive:** **radioactive iodine** (contraindicated in pregnancy/breastfeeding; may worsen thyroid eye disease; rendered hypothyroid) or **thyroidectomy** (large goitre, compressive symptoms, suspicious nodule, moderate–severe eye disease, patient preference); post-treatment levothyroxine.
  - **Toxic nodule/MNG:** radioiodine or surgery (ATDs do not induce remission).
  - **Thyroiditis:** NSAIDs/steroids for subacute pain; beta-blockers; transient (hypothyroid phase follows).
  - **Thyroid eye disease (Graves ophthalmopathy):** smoking cessation, selenium (mild), steroids (IV methylprednisolone), teprotumumab, orbital radiotherapy/decompression; urgent for **sight-threatening disease** (optic neuropathy, corneal exposure).
  - **Thyroid storm:** Chapter 43.
  - **Subclinical hyperthyroidism:** treat if TSH <0.1 and age >65, cardiac disease, osteoporosis, symptoms.
  - **Pregnancy:** PTU in first trimester then switch to carbimazole; TRAb monitoring (fetal thyrotoxicosis); postpartum thyroiditis.

#### Hypothyroidism

- **Causes:** **Hashimoto thyroiditis** (anti-TPO, anti-thyroglobulin antibodies; most common in iodine-sufficient regions), iodine deficiency (worldwide), **post-radioiodine/thyroidectomy,** **drugs** (amiodarone, lithium, checkpoint inhibitors, interferon, tyrosine kinase inhibitors), central (pituitary/hypothalamic disease), congenital, transient thyroiditis, infiltrative (rare).
- **Symptoms:** fatigue, **weight gain, cold intolerance, constipation, dry skin, hair loss, hoarse voice, bradycardia, delayed reflexes,** menorrhagia, depression, cognitive slowing, carpal tunnel syndrome, **hyperlipidaemia, hyponatraemia, raised CK, macrocytic anaemia,** pericardial and pleural effusions, myxoedema (non-pitting oedema); **myxoedema coma** (Chapter 43).
- **Treatment:** **levothyroxine** (full replacement 1.6 µg/kg/day; **start low (25–50 µg) in older adults and cardiac disease**), take on an empty stomach, 30–60 minutes before breakfast, separate from iron/calcium/PPIs; **recheck TSH 6–8 weeks** after dose change; target TSH within the reference range (0.4–4.0); **do not use T3 or desiccated thyroid** as routine; **subclinical hypothyroidism:** treat if TSH >10, symptomatic with positive TPO, pregnancy/planning pregnancy (**increase levothyroxine dose by 25–30% in pregnancy**), consider in young adults with TSH 4–10 and symptoms; avoid treating older adults (>70–80) with TSH <10.
- **Persistent symptoms on levothyroxine:** check adherence, absorption, dose, interactions (PPIs, coeliac), other diagnoses.

#### Goitre and thyroid nodules

- **Diffuse goitre:** Graves, Hashimoto, iodine deficiency, thyroiditis; **multinodular goitre** (compressive symptoms: dysphagia, stridor, Pemberton sign; retrosternal extension; surgery or radioiodine).
- **Solitary nodule:** ultrasound characterisation (TI-RADS), TSH, **FNA cytology (Bethesda)** for suspicious nodules; **thyroid cancer** (papillary 80–85%: excellent prognosis; follicular; medullary (calcitonin, RET, MEN2); anaplastic: emergency): total thyroidectomy ± radioiodine, TSH suppression, thyroglobulin follow-up (Chapter 31).

### 48.4 Adrenal disorders

#### Cushing syndrome

- **Causes:** **exogenous glucocorticoids (most common),** **ACTH-dependent** (pituitary adenoma = **Cushing disease** (≈70%), ectopic ACTH (small cell lung cancer, carcinoid)), **ACTH-independent** (adrenal adenoma/carcinoma, bilateral hyperplasia).
- **Features:** central obesity, **moon face, dorsocervical fat pad, purple striae (>1 cm), easy bruising, proximal myopathy, thin skin,** hypertension, diabetes, osteoporosis, hirsutism, menstrual disturbance, depression, hypokalaemia (ectopic), recurrent infections, poor wound healing, VTE.
- **Diagnosis:** exclude exogenous steroids; **screening (any two abnormal confirms):** **late-night salivary cortisol (×2),** **24-hour urinary free cortisol (×2),** **1-mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test** (cortisol >50 nmol/L (1.8 µg/dL) at 8–9 am the next morning is positive); **plasma ACTH** (suppressed <10 pg/mL: adrenal; normal/high: ACTH-dependent), **high-dose dexamethasone suppression/CRH test, MRI pituitary, inferior petrosal sinus sampling,** CT adrenals/chest-abdomen for ectopic source.
- **Treatment:** surgery (transsphenoidal pituitary surgery, adrenalectomy, resection of ectopic source), medical therapy (**metyrapone, osilodrostat, ketoconazole, mifepristone, pasireotide**), pituitary radiotherapy; **peri-operative glucocorticoid cover and post-operative adrenal insufficiency;** treat comorbidity (BP, diabetes, osteoporosis, VTE).

#### Primary aldosteronism (Conn syndrome)

(Chapter 45.) **Screen:** resistant/early hypertension, hypokalaemia, adrenal incidentaloma, sleep apnoea, family history: **aldosterone:renin ratio** (correct hypokalaemia; adjust interfering drugs: MRAs, beta-blockers, ACEi/ARBs, diuretics); confirmatory saline infusion/captopril/fludrocortisone suppression test; CT adrenals; **adrenal vein sampling** to lateralise (age <35 with typical features may skip); **adrenalectomy for unilateral** (curative), **MRA (spironolactone, eplerenone)** for bilateral hyperplasia.

#### Adrenal insufficiency

- **Primary (Addison disease):** autoimmune adrenalitis (commonest in developed countries; anti-21-hydroxylase antibodies; with other autoimmune disease), **tuberculosis,** adrenal haemorrhage (sepsis, anticoagulation, antiphospholipid syndrome), metastases, adrenoleukodystrophy, congenital adrenal hyperplasia, drugs (ketoconazole, etomidate, mitotane, checkpoint inhibitors).
- **Secondary/tertiary:** **exogenous glucocorticoid withdrawal (commonest cause overall),** pituitary/hypothalamic disease, pituitary surgery, head injury.
- **Features:** fatigue, weakness, **anorexia, weight loss, nausea, abdominal pain, postural hypotension, salt craving, hyperpigmentation (primary only),** hypoglycaemia, hyponatraemia, **hyperkalaemia (primary only: mineralocorticoid deficiency),** eosinophilia, raised TSH; **adrenal crisis** (Chapter 38, 43).
- **Diagnosis:** **8–9 a.m. serum cortisol** (<140 nmol/L (5 µg/dL) strongly suggests insufficiency; >400–500 makes it unlikely; 140–400 needs dynamic testing), **short synacthen (ACTH) test** (250 µg: cortisol >420–500 nmol/L at 30–60 minutes excludes primary; may be falsely normal in recent-onset secondary), **plasma ACTH** (high in primary), renin and aldosterone, anti-adrenal antibodies, adrenal CT.
- **Treatment:** **hydrocortisone** (15–25 mg/day in 2–3 divided doses, highest dose in the morning) **± fludrocortisone** (primary; 50–200 µg/day), **stress dosing** (sick-day rules: double dose with fever, triple with severe illness; IM hydrocortisone 100 mg in vomiting/trauma/surgery), **steroid emergency card/bracelet,** education, annual review; screen for associated autoimmune disease (thyroid, T1D, pernicious anaemia, coeliac).
- **Glucocorticoid-induced adrenal suppression:** any patient on ≥5 mg prednisolone equivalent for >3–4 weeks may be suppressed; **taper gradually, do not stop abruptly;** stress cover.

#### Phaeochromocytoma and paraganglioma

- **"Rule of 10s" is outdated;** about 40% are hereditary (MEN2, VHL, NF1, SDHx, TMEM127): **genetic testing for all.** **Features:** **paroxysmal hypertension, headache, sweating, palpitations,** pallor, anxiety, glucose intolerance, cardiomyopathy; can be asymptomatic (incidentaloma).
- **Diagnosis:** **plasma free metanephrines (supine, sampled after rest) or 24-hour urinary fractionated metanephrines** (very sensitive); interfering drugs (tricyclics, SNRIs, levodopa, labetalol, paracetamol assays); **CT/MRI** adrenals (and extra-adrenal), MIBG/PET (DOTATATE or FDG) for metastatic/extra-adrenal disease.
- **Treatment:** **alpha-blockade first (phenoxybenzamine or doxazosin) for 10–14 days, then beta-blocker** (never beta-blocker first: unopposed alpha stimulation causes hypertensive crisis), high-salt diet and fluids, then **laparoscopic adrenalectomy;** lifelong follow-up (recurrence/metastases), genetic counselling; hypertensive crisis: phentolamine.

#### Adrenal incidentaloma

- Mass ≥1 cm found incidentally on imaging (in about 3–5% of CT scans): **characterise** (unenhanced CT HU ≤10 suggests lipid-rich adenoma; size >4 cm, heterogeneous or irregular: suspicion for carcinoma or phaeochromocytoma), **biochemical screening in all** (**1-mg dexamethasone suppression test** for autonomous cortisol secretion, **plasma metanephrines** if not clearly an adenoma, **aldosterone:renin ratio** if hypertensive/hypokalaemic), surgery for functional tumours, malignancy suspected, or size >4–6 cm; follow-up imaging for indeterminate lesions.

#### Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH)

- **21-hydroxylase deficiency:** salt wasting in neonates (crisis: hypotension, hyponatraemia, hyperkalaemia, ambiguous genitalia in girls), virilisation, hirsutism, oligomenorrhoea in non-classic forms; raised 17-hydroxyprogesterone; glucocorticoid ± fludrocortisone replacement.

### 48.5 Pituitary disorders

#### Hypopituitarism

- **Causes:** **pituitary adenoma (macroadenoma),** pituitary surgery/radiotherapy, traumatic brain injury, **apoplexy,** **Sheehan syndrome** (post-partum haemorrhage), **hypophysitis (checkpoint inhibitors),** craniopharyngioma, infiltrative (sarcoid, haemochromatosis, histiocytosis), empty sella.
- **Features:** fatigue, weight loss, hypotension (ACTH), cold intolerance (TSH), **loss of libido, amenorrhoea/infertility (LH/FSH), loss of body hair, pallor,** hypoglycaemia, hyponatraemia, **diabetes insipidus** (posterior pituitary); in adults GH deficiency (reduced muscle mass, quality of life); headache and **bitemporal hemianopia** if mass effect.
- **Order of loss:** GH and gonadotrophins first, then TSH, then ACTH (prolactin may be high with stalk compression).
- **Investigations:** **8 a.m. cortisol, ACTH, free T4 with TSH (low or "inappropriately normal"), LH, FSH, oestradiol/testosterone, prolactin, IGF-1, sodium and osmolality;** dynamic tests (synacthen, insulin tolerance test or glucagon test for GH/ACTH), **MRI pituitary.**
- **Treatment:** **replace hormones** (hydrocortisone first, then levothyroxine, sex steroids, growth hormone in selected adults, desmopressin for DI); **always replace glucocorticoid before thyroxine;** treat the cause; **pituitary apoplexy:** emergency (headache, visual loss, ophthalmoplegia, hypotension): IV hydrocortisone, neurosurgery.
- **Diabetes insipidus:** polyuria, thirst; central (low ADH) vs nephrogenic; water deprivation test/copeptin; desmopressin (watch for hyponatraemia).

#### Hyperprolactinaemia and prolactinoma

- **Causes:** **prolactinoma** (most common functioning pituitary adenoma), **drugs** (antipsychotics, metoclopramide, domperidone, antidepressants, opiates, verapamil), **pregnancy/lactation,** primary hypothyroidism, **stalk compression ("stalk effect")** from non-functioning adenomas, renal failure, PCOS, chest wall stimulation, macroprolactin.
- **Features:** women: **galactorrhoea, oligo/amenorrhoea, infertility;** men: **reduced libido, erectile dysfunction, infertility, gynaecomastia;** mass effect (headache, visual field loss).
- **Diagnosis:** repeated prolactin (>2,000 mU/L (≈100 ng/mL) suggests prolactinoma; <2,000 consider drugs/stalk effect/macroprolactin), TSH, pregnancy test, renal function, **MRI pituitary.**
- **Treatment:** **dopamine agonists** (**cabergoline** first-line, bromocriptine) shrink tumours and normalise prolactin in most; monitor for impulse-control disorders and valvulopathy (high doses), discontinue after 2 years in selected patients with normalised prolactin and no tumour; **surgery** for resistant or intolerant; pregnancy planning (cabergoline stopped; microprolactinomas rarely enlarge).

#### Acromegaly

- **Growth hormone excess** (pituitary adenoma): **enlarged hands/feet, coarse facial features, prognathism, macroglossia, carpal tunnel syndrome, sweating, headaches, sleep apnoea, hypertension, diabetes, cardiomyopathy, arthropathy, colon polyps/cancer, visual field loss;** diagnosis delayed by years.
- **Diagnosis:** **raised IGF-1;** **oral glucose tolerance test with GH measurement** (GH not suppressed to <1 µg/L (0.4 in sensitive assays)); MRI pituitary.
- **Treatment:** **transsphenoidal surgery** (first-line), **somatostatin analogues (octreotide, lanreotide, pasireotide),** pegvisomant (GH receptor antagonist), cabergoline, radiotherapy; screen for complications and colonoscopy.

#### Non-functioning pituitary adenomas and other sellar masses

- Pituitary incidentaloma (≥10 mm: macroadenoma): assess hormones and visual fields; surgery for visual compromise; microadenomas (<10 mm): hormonal screen and follow-up MRI. **Craniopharyngioma, Rathke cleft cyst, meningioma, hypophysitis.**

### 48.6 Calcium, parathyroid and bone disorders

#### Primary hyperparathyroidism

- Commonest cause of outpatient hypercalcaemia: **adenoma (85%),** hyperplasia, carcinoma (rare); MEN1 and MEN2A; **hypercalcaemia with raised or inappropriately normal PTH.** Often asymptomatic; "stones, bones, groans, moans" (Chapter 43); osteoporosis, nephrolithiasis, hypertension, neurocognitive symptoms.
- **Work-up:** corrected calcium, PTH, 25-OH vitamin D, creatinine/eGFR, 24-hour urine calcium (exclude **familial hypocalciuric hypercalcaemia,** FHH: low urinary calcium/creatinine clearance ratio <0.01), DXA, renal imaging for stones.
- **Parathyroidectomy** if symptomatic, **calcium >0.25 mmol/L above upper limit,** age <50, eGFR <60, osteoporosis/fragility fracture, nephrolithiasis/nephrocalcinosis; localisation (ultrasound, sestamibi scan, 4D-CT); observation or **cinacalcet/bisphosphonate** if not surgical.
- **Secondary hyperparathyroidism:** CKD, vitamin D deficiency; **tertiary:** post-transplant.

#### Vitamin D deficiency, osteomalacia and rickets

- **Causes:** reduced sun exposure, dark skin, dietary deficiency, malabsorption, CKD, liver disease, anticonvulsants; **osteomalacia** (bone pain, proximal weakness, fractures, low calcium/phosphate, raised ALP, low 25-OH D; Looser zones); **rickets** in children (bowing, rachitic rosary).
- **Treatment:** **cholecalciferol** (e.g. loading 50,000 IU weekly for 6–8 weeks or 3,000–6,000 IU daily, then maintenance 800–2,000 IU/day), calcium intake, treat cause; check 25-OH D in at-risk groups.

#### Osteoporosis

- **Definition:** low bone mass and microarchitectural deterioration with increased fracture risk; **DXA T-score ≤ −2.5** (at hip or spine; WHO) **or fragility fracture** (hip, vertebral) regardless of BMD.
- **Risk factors:** age, female sex, postmenopause, low BMI, family history (hip fracture), previous fracture, **glucocorticoids (≥5 mg prednisolone for ≥3 months),** smoking, alcohol, hypogonadism, hyperparathyroidism, hyperthyroidism, coeliac disease, IBD, RA, CKD, aromatase inhibitors, androgen-deprivation therapy, PPIs, anticonvulsants, glitazones, falls.
- **Assessment:** **FRAX** (10-year probability of major osteoporotic and hip fracture), **DXA,** vertebral fracture assessment (height loss >4 cm, kyphosis), **secondary causes screen** (calcium, PTH, vitamin D, TSH, creatinine, LFTs, coeliac serology, protein electrophoresis, testosterone in men).
- **Treatment:**
  - **General:** calcium (1,000–1,200 mg/day total, preferably dietary), **vitamin D** (800–1,000 IU/day), **weight-bearing and resistance exercise,** falls prevention, stop smoking, alcohol moderation.
  - **Pharmacological** (high risk: fragility fracture, T-score ≤ −2.5, FRAX above intervention threshold): **oral bisphosphonates** (alendronate 70 mg weekly, risedronate) or **zoledronic acid 5 mg IV yearly;** **denosumab** 60 mg SC every 6 months (must not stop abruptly: rebound vertebral fractures; bridge with bisphosphonate); **anabolic agents** (**teriparatide, abaloparatide, romosozumab**) for very high risk (multiple vertebral fractures, very low T-score); **raloxifene, HRT** (selected postmenopausal women).
  - **Safety:** atypical femoral fractures and osteonecrosis of the jaw (rare; dental review), acute-phase reaction with IV bisphosphonates, upper GI effects of oral drugs (take upright with water, fasting), hypocalcaemia (ensure vitamin D replete).
  - **Duration:** bisphosphonate 3–5 years (oral 5; IV 3), drug holiday if low risk after treatment; reassess.
  - **Men and glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis:** treat per risk (alendronate/zoledronate/teriparatide).
- **Paget disease of bone:** focal disorder of bone remodelling; bone pain, deformity, deafness, high ALP with normal calcium; zoledronate for symptomatic or high-risk sites.

### 48.7 Obesity and metabolic syndrome

- **Definition:** BMI ≥30 kg/m² (≥27.5 in South Asian populations for health risk); waist circumference (>94 cm men, >80 cm women in Europeans) and adiposity-based assessments; **obesity is a chronic relapsing disease.**
- **Causes and contributors:** energy imbalance, genetics (monogenic: leptin, MC4R), **endocrine** (hypothyroidism, Cushing, hypothalamic, PCOS, insulin resistance), **drugs** (antipsychotics, antidepressants (mirtazapine, paroxetine), antiepileptics (valproate), insulin, sulfonylureas, glucocorticoids, beta-blockers), sleep apnoea, binge-eating disorder, depression, socioeconomic factors.
- **Complications:** type 2 diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidaemia, cardiovascular disease, **MASLD,** sleep apnoea, osteoarthritis, GORD, cancers (endometrial, breast, colon, kidney, oesophageal), infertility/PCOS, depression, stigma, VTE.
- **Management:**
  1. **Non-judgemental assessment,** weight history, previous attempts, barriers, mental health, medications; **screen for secondary causes only when clinically suggested.**
  2. **Lifestyle:** nutrition (reduced-energy, high-quality diet), physical activity, behavioural therapy, sleep.
  3. **Pharmacotherapy** (BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with comorbidity): **semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly** (≈15% weight loss), **tirzepatide** (≈20%), liraglutide 3 mg, orlistat, naltrexone–bupropion, phentermine–topiramate (availability varies); benefits in cardiovascular, MASLD, HFpEF, OSA; adverse effects (GI, gallstones, pancreatitis risk, muscle loss, regain on stopping; avoid in pregnancy; contraception counselling).
  4. **Bariatric/metabolic surgery** (sleeve gastrectomy, Roux-en-Y gastric bypass): BMI ≥40 (≥35 with comorbidity; ≥30 with diabetes in some guidelines): 25–35% weight loss, diabetes remission, reduced mortality; lifelong micronutrient supplementation (B12, iron, calcium, vitamin D), nutritional follow-up; **complications** (dumping, anastomotic leak, internal hernia, marginal ulcer, deficiencies, hypoglycaemia).
- **Metabolic syndrome:** central obesity plus ≥2 of raised triglycerides, low HDL, hypertension, raised fasting glucose; identifies cardiometabolic risk.
- **Eating disorders:** screen (binge-eating disorder is common in obesity); avoid stigmatising language.

### 48.8 Dyslipidaemia

- **Screening and risk assessment:** lipid profile (non-fasting acceptable), **cardiovascular risk calculators (QRISK, SCORE2, ASCVD risk)**, lipoprotein(a) (once in lifetime), apolipoprotein B.
- **Secondary causes:** hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome, cholestasis, diabetes, obesity, alcohol, drugs (steroids, thiazides, beta-blockers, retinoids, oestrogens, protease inhibitors), pregnancy.
- **Familial hypercholesterolaemia (FH):** LDL-C >5 mmol/L (190 mg/dL), tendon xanthomata, premature coronary disease, family history; **heterozygous prevalence 1:250;** genetic testing, **cascade screening;** high-intensity statin + ezetimibe ± PCSK9 inhibitor/inclisiran; start in childhood.
- **Hypertriglyceridaemia:** >10 mmol/L risks pancreatitis: lifestyle, fibrates, omega-3 (icosapent ethyl reduces events in high-risk), treat diabetes and alcohol.
- **Treatment:** **statins** (atorvastatin 20–80 mg, rosuvastatin 10–40 mg) reduce events by about 20–25% per 1 mmol/L LDL-C reduction; **targets** (ESC): **LDL-C <1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) with ≥50% reduction for very high risk; <1.0 mmol/L (40 mg/dL) with recurrent events;** add **ezetimibe,** then **PCSK9 monoclonal antibodies (evolocumab, alirocumab) or inclisiran,** **bempedoic acid;** monitor ALT at baseline and as indicated, muscle symptoms (check CK, TSH, vitamin D; alternative statin or dose; intolerance is often nocebo), **statins in diabetes, CKD, primary prevention by risk,** and in older adults by individual benefit.

### 48.9 Gout and disorders of purine metabolism (summary)

(See Chapter 29.) **Gout** (monosodium urate crystals; hyperuricaemia; first MTP): acute flare treatment (NSAIDs, colchicine, steroids), **urate-lowering therapy (allopurinol titrated to urate <360 µmol/L (6 mg/dL); febuxostat; benzbromarone)** in recurrent gout, tophi, CKD, urate stones, with flare prophylaxis for 3–6 months; lifestyle and treat comorbidities (hypertension, CKD, metabolic syndrome); losartan and fenofibrate lower urate modestly. **Hyperuricaemia alone is not treated** unless extreme or associated with stones/CKD/tumour lysis.

### 48.10 Other endocrine topics (brief)

- **Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS):** oligo-anovulation, hyperandrogenism (hirsutism, acne), polycystic ovaries (two of three, Rotterdam), insulin resistance; exclude thyroid disease, hyperprolactinaemia, **non-classic CAH,** androgen-secreting tumours, Cushing; **lifestyle and weight loss,** metformin, **combined oral contraceptive** (menstrual regularity, hirsutism), anti-androgens (spironolactone), letrozole for ovulation induction, screening for diabetes, dyslipidaemia, sleep apnoea, endometrial protection.
- **Hypogonadism (men):** low testosterone (morning, repeated) with symptoms (low libido, erectile dysfunction, fatigue, reduced muscle mass, osteoporosis): **LH/FSH** distinguish primary (high; Klinefelter, testicular damage) from secondary (low/normal; pituitary disease, obesity, opioids, anabolic steroids); testosterone replacement (check PSA/haematocrit; avoid with fertility desire, prostate cancer, polycythaemia).
- **Menopause and premature ovarian insufficiency:** Chapter 19; HRT benefits and risks.
- **Multiple endocrine neoplasia:** **MEN1** (parathyroid, pituitary, pancreatic NETs), **MEN2A** (medullary thyroid cancer, phaeochromocytoma, hyperparathyroidism), **MEN2B**: genetic testing and surveillance.
- **Hyponatraemia, SIADH:** Chapter 43. **Hypoglycaemia:** Chapter 43. **Gynaecomastia:** drugs (spironolactone, cimetidine, antipsychotics), cirrhosis, hypogonadism, tumours.
- **Inborn errors of metabolism** (phenylketonuria, galactosaemia, urea cycle defects, fatty acid oxidation disorders) present with neonatal collapse, poor feeding, hypoglycaemia, acidosis, hyperammonaemia: **neonatal screening and urgent metabolic evaluation.**
- **Porphyria, amyloidosis, haemochromatosis, Wilson** (Chapter 47).
- **Neuroendocrine tumours/carcinoid syndrome:** flushing, diarrhoea, wheeze, right heart valve disease; **24-hour urine 5-HIAA,** chromogranin A, imaging (DOTATATE PET), somatostatin analogues.

### 48.11 Worked cases

#### Case 1: New type 2 diabetes with cardiovascular risk

*A 58-year-old man, BMI 33, hypertension, previous MI, has fasting glucose 9.2 mmol/L and HbA1c 68 mmol/mol (8.4%). eGFR 72, uACR 6 mg/mmol.*

**Reasoning.** **Type 2 diabetes with established ASCVD and albuminuria.**

**Action.** Education and lifestyle; **metformin** plus an **SGLT2 inhibitor and/or GLP-1 receptor agonist** for cardiorenal protection (independent of HbA1c); high-intensity statin, ACEi/ARB, BP target <130/80, antiplatelet, annual retinal/foot/kidney screening, consider weight-loss agent (semaglutide/tirzepatide), review HbA1c at 3 months.

#### Case 2: Graves' disease

*A 32-year-old woman has 4 months of weight loss, palpitations, tremor and heat intolerance. HR 112 AF absent, diffuse goitre with bruit, lid lag. TSH <0.01, free T4 48 pmol/L, TRAb positive. She is not pregnant but wants a family in 2 years.*

**Reasoning.** **Graves' hyperthyroidism.**

**Action.** Propranolol for symptoms; **carbimazole (block-and-replace or titration) for 12–18 months,** warn about agranulocytosis, monitor TFTs; discuss definitive therapy (radioiodine or thyroidectomy) before pregnancy planning; **propylthiouracil in the first trimester;** eye assessment; stop smoking.

#### Case 3: Adrenal insufficiency

*A 34-year-old woman with type 1 diabetes and autoimmune thyroid disease has 6 months of fatigue, weight loss, nausea, dizziness and tanning of the skin creases. BP 92/58 lying, 76/50 standing. Na 128, K 5.8, glucose 3.5 mmol/L. 9 a.m. cortisol 60 nmol/L; ACTH very high; anti-21-hydroxylase positive.*

**Reasoning.** **Autoimmune primary adrenal insufficiency (Addison disease).**

**Action.** **Hydrocortisone replacement (e.g. 15–20 mg/day in divided doses) plus fludrocortisone;** sick-day rules, emergency hydrocortisone injection kit, steroid card; reduce insulin as glucose requirements change; adrenal crisis education; annual review.

#### Case 4: Cushing syndrome

*A 45-year-old woman has weight gain, new hypertension and diabetes, purple striae, easy bruising and proximal weakness. Late-night salivary cortisol is raised on two occasions; 1-mg dexamethasone suppression: cortisol 280 nmol/L; ACTH 58 pg/mL (inappropriately normal–high). MRI: 6-mm pituitary lesion.*

**Reasoning.** **ACTH-dependent Cushing syndrome (Cushing disease).**

**Action.** Confirm with high-dose dexamethasone/CRH, **inferior petrosal sinus sampling** if imaging equivocal, **transsphenoidal surgery,** peri-operative steroid cover, then lifelong assessment for adrenal insufficiency; treat BP, glucose, bone, VTE risk.

#### Case 5: Prolactinoma

*A 28-year-old woman has 8 months of amenorrhoea and galactorrhoea. Pregnancy test negative, TSH normal; prolactin 5,200 mU/L (repeated). MRI: 7-mm pituitary microadenoma.*

**Reasoning.** **Microprolactinoma.**

**Action.** **Cabergoline** (start low; titrate), repeat prolactin and MRI later; contraception discussion; plan pregnancy (stop cabergoline when pregnant; monitor visual symptoms); review at 2 years for withdrawal.

#### Case 6: Postmenopausal osteoporosis

*A 67-year-old woman has a wrist fracture after a fall from standing, T-score −3.0 at the spine, FRAX hip 5.1%. Calcium, vitamin D and creatinine normal after replacement; coeliac serology negative.*

**Reasoning.** **Osteoporosis with fragility fracture:** treat.

**Action.** **Alendronate** (or zoledronic acid), calcium/vitamin D, exercise, falls prevention, dental review before starting, reassess in 3–5 years; consider anabolic therapy if very high risk or multiple fractures.

#### Case 7: Primary hyperparathyroidism

*A 62-year-old woman with fatigue and a renal stone has corrected calcium 2.88 mmol/L, PTH 12 pmol/L (raised), 25-OH vitamin D 62 nmol/L, urine calcium excretion high; DXA T-score −2.7 at the forearm.*

**Reasoning.** **Primary hyperparathyroidism** (symptomatic: stones, osteoporosis).

**Action.** **Parathyroidectomy** after localisation (ultrasound and sestamibi); hydration, vitamin D repletion; post-operative calcium monitoring (hungry bone syndrome); family and MEN screening if young or multiglandular.

### 48.12 Common pitfalls

- **Diagnosing diabetes on one abnormal asymptomatic test;** misusing HbA1c in haemoglobinopathies/pregnancy; missing LADA/MODY in lean young patients labelled type 2.
- **Treating T2D only by glucose** (ignoring cardiorenal-protective drugs, BP, lipids, weight).
- **Starting levothyroxine for abnormal TFTs during acute illness** (non-thyroidal illness) or treating subclinical hypothyroidism without criteria; **giving levothyroxine before glucocorticoid** in suspected adrenal insufficiency.
- **Failing to check TSH in unexplained AF, depression, osteoporosis, dyslipidaemia, infertility.**
- **Using beta-blockers before alpha-blockade in phaeochromocytoma.**
- **Interpreting a normal random cortisol** as excluding adrenal insufficiency; stopping steroids abruptly.
- **Missing adrenal incidentaloma work-up** (cortisol, metanephrines, aldosterone).
- **Ordering IGF-1/cortisol/prolactin "just in case"** without pre-test probability.
- **Stopping denosumab abruptly;** neglecting vitamin D repletion before antiresorptives.
- **Missing secondary osteoporosis causes** (coeliac, hyperparathyroidism, myeloma, steroids).
- **Stigmatising language in obesity;** not offering evidence-based medication or surgery.
- **Overlooking lipid targets and lipoprotein(a);** stopping statins for non-specific symptoms without evaluation.

### 48.13 Key points

- Diagnose diabetes by glucose/HbA1c criteria with confirmation; type 2 management is cardiorenal-protective (metformin + SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA), weight-centred, with annual complication screening; type 1 needs basal-bolus insulin with CGM/closed-loop where possible.
- Interpret TFTs by the TSH–free T4 pattern; treat Graves with antithyroid drugs, radioiodine or surgery; treat hypothyroidism with levothyroxine titrated to TSH; evaluate nodules by ultrasound and FNA.
- Adrenal disease: screen with the appropriate test (dexamethasone suppression/late-night cortisol; aldosterone:renin ratio; metanephrines; 9 a.m. cortisol and synacthen), localise after biochemistry, and always cover surgery/illness with steroids in insufficiency.
- Pituitary: assess all axes, replace glucocorticoid first, dopamine agonists for prolactinoma, surgery for acromegaly and Cushing disease.
- Calcium and bone: primary hyperparathyroidism (surgery if criteria), vitamin D deficiency, osteoporosis (FRAX/DXA, bisphosphonates/denosumab/anabolics).
- Obesity is a disease: lifestyle plus GLP-1/dual agonists or bariatric surgery; dyslipidaemia: statins with LDL targets and add-ons.

### 48.14 Self-assessment

1. State the diagnostic thresholds for diabetes by fasting glucose, 2-hour OGTT, and HbA1c.
   *Answer: Fasting glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L, 2-hour OGTT ≥11.1 mmol/L, HbA1c ≥48 mmol/mol (6.5%); random glucose ≥11.1 with symptoms; confirm if asymptomatic.*

2. Which drug classes are recommended in type 2 diabetes with established cardiovascular disease or CKD, independent of HbA1c?
   *Answer: SGLT2 inhibitors and/or GLP-1 receptor agonists (with metformin as appropriate).*

3. Interpret: TSH 0.01, free T4 high, TRAb positive, diffuse goitre with bruit.
   *Answer: Graves' hyperthyroidism; treat with beta-blocker, antithyroid drugs, then consider radioiodine or surgery.*

4. How is adrenal insufficiency diagnosed and what must be replaced?
   *Answer: Low 8–9 a.m. cortisol and/or failed synacthen test with high ACTH (primary); hydrocortisone replacement (plus fludrocortisone in primary), with stress dosing and steroid card.*

5. What is the order of therapy in phaeochromocytoma?
   *Answer: Alpha-blockade (phenoxybenzamine or doxazosin) first, then beta-blockade, then surgery; never beta-blocker first.*

6. List first-line pharmacological options for osteoporosis and one safety concern for each.
   *Answer: Bisphosphonates (oesophageal irritation, atypical femoral fracture, osteonecrosis of the jaw), denosumab (rebound vertebral fractures if stopped), anabolic agents (teriparatide/abaloparatide/romosozumab for very high risk; cardiovascular caution with romosozumab).*
